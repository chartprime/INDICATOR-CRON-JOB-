# Build brief: Relative Volume by Time of Day

PM brief for the cycle-1 build. Shared rules: PM/WORKER-BRIEF.md. Capability
sheet: PM/CAPABILITIES.md. UX standard: PM/cycle1/ux-standard.md (follow it
where it exists; where it is silent, follow the house conventions in the
existing indicators). Validation protocol: PM/cycle1/validation-protocol.md.

## The trading question

"Is this bar's volume unusual for this time of day, or is it just the open,
the close or the London/New York overlap being its normal self?"

## Target user and decision

Intraday traders on ES, NQ, AAPL, TSLA, BTC and ETH who confirm moves with
volume. Decision: treat participation as genuinely unusual for this minute,
or discount it because this minute is always loud. High relative volume on
its own says nothing about direction or follow-through, and the package says
so. Plain rolling
RVOL fires every morning at the open and goes quiet every lunch; this tool
stays flat where the clock explains the volume and lights up only when the bar
is unusual for its slot.

## Identity

- Folder `apps/web/src/lib/indicators/relative-volume/`, def `relativeVolume.ts`,
  computation in `relativeVolumeMath.ts` (pure, testable), tests in `tests/`.
- id `relativeVolume`, label `"Relative Volume (Time of Day)"`, category
  `"technical"`, placement `{ kind: "ownPane", priceLines: [1, 2, 3] }`.
- Historical Mode policy `OHLCV_POLICY`. Works on every archived instrument
  and on equities (unsided volume is irrelevant: only `bar.volume` is read).
- No `timeframeGate` (it would remove the indicator and its table entirely).
  For `interval_ms > 3_600_000` every output is `null` and the status row says
  `Time of day needs an intraday timeframe`.

## Data

`bar.ts_open`, `bar.interval_ms`, `bar.volume`, `bar.closed`, `bar.provider`,
`bar.assetClass`, `bar.marketKind`. Nothing else. No wall clock. Identity
fields choose the clock convention only; availability is governed by the
OHLCV capability policy, never by provider. A bar whose `volume` is not a
finite number >= 0 contributes no sample and yields `null` for that bar.
Missing bars (gaps in `ts_open`) contribute nothing, never a zero sample.

Math library note: the `ta.*` factories are top-level imports
(`import { ta } from "../../chart/ta"`) created once inside `createState`,
exactly as `docs/architecture/indicator-sdk/TA.02-ta-math-library-evidence.md`
describes; `ctx.ta` is an intentionally empty namespace and is not used. The
rolling comparison uses `ta.sma(20)` fed with committed volumes only (see the
commit rule), so its denominator excludes the current bar, mirroring the slot
method; `null` until 20 committed bars.

## Calculation (incremental, one bar at a time)

1. **Clock.** Equities and futures (provider DXFEED or DATABENTO): New York
   local minute of day, computed the way `killzones` does it (reuse
   `apps/web/src/lib/chart/marketCalendar.ts` or the `nyParts` helper; do not
   write a new time-zone routine). Crypto (provider BINANCE): UTC minute of
   day, with two slot families, weekday and weekend, selected by the UTC day of
   week. Advanced setting `clock`: `auto` (default), `newYork`, `utc`.
2. **Slot key.** The bar's start minute of day in the resolved clock
   (0..1439), plus the family. No division by bar length: an hourly equity
   bar starting 09:30 keys 570, the half-hour 15:30 bar keys 930, and neither
   collides with another slot. At most 1,440 keys per family.
3. **Observation = volume rate.** What enters a ring is
   `rate = volume / (interval_ms / 60_000)`, volume per minute, so a short bar
   (an early-close day, the final equity hour bar) is compared with full bars
   fairly. The forming bar's rate uses its declared `interval_ms`.
4. **Baseline.** Per slot key, a ring of the last `sessions` committed
   observations `{ts_open, rate}` (default 10, range 2..60) and their median.
   Median, not mean, so one news day does not inflate tomorrow's baseline. An
   observation is evicted when it is older than the chart's retention window,
   `bar.ts_open - 10_080 * bar.interval_ms`, so state never depends on bars
   the chart no longer holds; a REBUILT replay from retained bars then
   reproduces live state exactly.
5. **Baseline read before write.** For the bar being evaluated, the slot
   median and the rolling SMA are read first (`baselineAtOpen`), stored on the
   bar's own record, and every later recomputation of that bar (a `replace`
   revision, a duplicate closed dispatch) divides by that stored value. A bar
   never sees its own volume in its denominator.
6. **Commit rule (one observation per `ts_open`, correctable).** State keeps
   the current bar record `{ts_open, rate, baselineAtOpen, committed}`. The
   observation is inserted into its slot ring and into the rolling window at
   the first of: (a) the first dispatch of that `ts_open` with
   `bar.closed === true`; (b) the arrival of a bar with a greater `ts_open`
   while the record is uncommitted (a close that never arrived), inserting the
   last known rate. Rings store `{ts_open, rate}`, so a later closed dispatch
   of the same `ts_open` with a different volume UPDATES that observation in
   place (same key, new rate) and recomputes the bar's own ratio from its
   stored `baselineAtOpen`; it never inserts twice. This keeps live state equal
   to a replay that only ever saw the corrected bar. A dispatch older than the
   current record is ignored. The replay tail may be a forming bar (the
   runtime keeps the supplied `closed` flags); it stays pending like any live
   forming bar.
7. **Confirmation.** A bar's outputs and markers are provisional while
   `bar.closed === false` (drawn lighter) and confirmed on the first closed
   dispatch, including the last bar of a replay. No bar ever emits a marker
   for a different bar: a marker is always for the bar of the current call,
   so the runtime's per-timestamp replace cannot leave stale or duplicate
   markers.
8. **Output.** `rvol = rate / slotMedian`, `null` while the slot has fewer
   than `minSessions` (default 3) observations or the median is 0 or not
   finite. Median of an even count is the mean of the two middle values. The
   forming bar is divided by the full-slot expectation, so it grows toward its
   final value during the bar. `rolling = rate / mean of the previous 20
   committed rates`, `null` during its warm-up. `minSessions` is clamped to
   `<= sessions` at read time. Precedence of states: invalid current volume
   gives `No usable volume` even while the slot is still learning.
9. **Markers (alert conditions).** `hot`: `rvol >= hotThreshold` (default 2)
   draws a small circle on that bar; `rvol >= 3` the same circle, larger.
   `sustained` (advanced, off by default): a committed run counter advances
   only when a bar commits with `rvol >= sustainedLevel`; the forming bar is
   evaluated provisionally as `committedRun + (formingQualifies ? 1 : 0)` and
   never advances the committed counter, so repeated forming updates cannot
   inflate the run. The marker is drawn on the bar at which the provisional
   run first reaches `sustainedBars` (default 3), once per run. The run resets
   on a bar below the level, a `null` or invalid bar, a missing bar in the
   sequence (next `ts_open` not equal to previous plus `interval_ms`), or a
   session boundary (a change of calendar day in the resolved clock, or of
   family). Markers are derived from state on every call, so a replace
   redraws rather than duplicates.
10. **Sessions, slots and duration.** A ring holds observations, one per
   committed bar; because a slot occurs once per calendar session, a session
   contributes at most one sample per slot. Exception: the repeated New York
   hour at the autumn DST change can land two futures bars in one slot; both
   are kept as observations, documented, no special case. Bars shorter than
   their nominal length (the 15:30 to 16:00 equity hour bar, an early-close
   day's last bar) are handled by the rate observation and the start-minute
   key: a shortened bar is compared per minute with other bars that started at
   the same clock minute. `sessions` is the ring capacity, not
   a promise of available history: retained history supplies what it has, and
   the table shows the actual sample count.
11. **No future data.** Every value at bar t uses only bars with
    `ts_open <= t.ts_open`. Truncating the series at any index must not change
    earlier outputs or earlier markers (the validator checks both).
12. **Fail closed.** Any non-finite intermediate yields `null`.

## Default view (what a trader sees in three seconds)

Own pane. One line `rvol` with `ctx.draw.fill(rvol, 1, ...)`: green
(`#26a69a`, alpha) above 1, grey below 1. Line color per bar: hot (>= hot
threshold) `#ff9800`, above 1 `#26a69a`, below 1 `#787b86`. Price lines at
1, 2 and 3. Hot markers on. The rolling comparison line is OFF by default
(the orange line already identifies hot bars); its value still shows in the
table so the contrast is one glance away. Table, top right, three rows:
`RVOL 2.4x (time of day, n=7)` colored like the line, where n is the slot's
actual observation count; `1.1x (rolling 20)`; a status row that is exactly
one of `Forming: versus full-bar expectation` (bar not closed),
`Learning this time of day: 1 of 3 sessions`, `No usable volume` (invalid
current volume), `No usable volume baseline` (median 0), `Time of day needs an
intraday timeframe`, or blank when ready and closed. Before warm-up the
pane is empty, never zero.

## Baseline to beat, and the evidence required

Baseline: `volume / SMA(20)` of the previous 20 committed bars (the
comparison line itself). Both methods are evaluated at the same threshold
(2.0), on the same bars (those where both are ready), and both recall and
false-positive rate are reported for both without a predetermined winner.

Deterministic equity fixture (in the validation harness): 15 sessions of
390 one-minute bars, New York 09:30 to 16:00. Expected volume for minute m
(0..389) with midday level V = 1,000: `E(m) = V * (1 + 3 * max(0, 1 - m/60)^2)`
for m < 60, `V` for 60 <= m <= 330, `V * (1 + (m - 330) / 59)` for m > 330
(so 4.0x at the open decaying to 1.0x by minute 60, flat, rising to 2.0x at
the close). Noise: `volume = E(m) * exp(0.15 * z)` with z standard normal from
a Box-Muller transform over mulberry32 seeded 20261026. Anomalies: 12 distinct
(session, minute) pairs drawn with the same generator from sessions 4..15 and
minutes 90..330, each replaced by `2.5 * E(m)` (the expected, not the noisy,
value). Evaluation bars: sessions 4..15 where both methods are ready.
"Predictable slots" are minutes 0..9 and 380..389. A false positive is a
detection on an eligible non-anomaly bar; report the overall rate and the
predictable-slot rate.

Deterministic crypto fixture for performance and retention: 10,080 one-minute
bars (7 UTC days), expected volume `V * (1 + 0.5 * cos(2 * pi * (m - 840) /
1440))` with the same noise model, seed 20261027. Used for the p99 timing,
the rollover (feed 12,000 bars, confirm bounded state), and a REBUILT replay
from the last 10,080 bars that must reproduce the live outputs for every bar
whose full lookback lies within retention.

Acceptance for this indicator (descriptive, not predictive):
- Recall on the 12 anomalies >= 11/12.
- Predictable-slot false-positive rate <= 10 percent of those bars.
- The rolling method's recall, overall and predictable-slot false-positive
  rates on the same bars are reported beside these in the doc page, whatever
  they are. The hypothesis
  under test is that the time-of-day method has a lower false-positive rate
  at the open and close with comparable recall; if the fixture disproves it,
  the brief returns to the PM.

Additional fixtures with expected outcomes:
- Weekend split ON on the 7-day crypto fixture: weekend slots show
  `Learning: 2 of 3 sessions`, weekday slots are ready from day 4 on; no NaN.
- New York DST week for futures (autumn): the repeated 01:00 hour yields two
  observations in those slot keys that day, both kept; the spring week yields
  none for the skipped hour; outputs identical live and in replay.
- Gap: a session missing minutes 120..179 contributes no observations for
  those keys; the bar after the gap has an unchanged baseline and a reset
  sustained run.
- Early close at 13:00: the 12:30 hourly bar (30 minutes) is compared per
  minute with other 12:30 bars; its ratio is near 1 when its rate is normal.
- Changed-volume closed replacement: a bar closes at 100, then closes again at
  150; the ring observation becomes 150, that bar's own ratio uses its stored
  denominator, and every later bar matches a replay that only saw 150.
- Duplicate identical closed dispatch: no change in any output.
- Replay with a forming tail bar: the tail stays pending and provisional.

## Limitations to state in the package

Needs `minSessions` prior sessions per slot: one-minute crypto retains seven
UTC days, one-minute equities about 25 sessions; a fresh symbol shows
"learning" for three sessions. Holidays and early closes are ordinary sessions
in this version. Equity candles are regular session only. The ratio is
unit-free; volume units are the provider's.

# Data for indicator authors

[Handbook](../../README.md) · [Indicator index](README.md) ·
[Onboarding](../../operations/00-developer-onboarding.md) ·
[Indicator SDK](../indicator-sdk/TA.01-indicator-authoring-core-evidence.md)

An indicator never opens SQLite, S3 or a provider connection itself. It reads
what the runtime and the archive deliver through a small number of supported
boundaries. This page names those boundaries, says what each one carries for
each instrument, and lists the rules every indicator has to follow. The code
named here is the authority; when this page and the code disagree, the code
wins and this page is the defect.

## Three sources of data

| Source | How it reaches an indicator | You need | When it is missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Live ticks** | Terminal C holds the provider session and sends canonical frames (`bar`, `trade`, `book`, `health`, profiles, VPIN readings) over the local `/ws` WebSocket | Binance: nothing. dxFeed: layer 2 (Tailscale broker) | dxFeed instruments show *History only*: with layer 1 their recorded host history still loads, without new ticks |
| **Warm-up** | Before live ticks arrive, the runtime reads recent history through its storage reader: candles, large trades, aggressive prints, volume profile, VPIN calibration | Layer 1 (WireGuard + bearer); otherwise only a local collector's SQLite | The indicator starts from live ticks only and reports itself not ready |
| **Archive** | Historical Mode reads candles through `/archive/query` and asks the host for four studies through `/archive/study` | Layer 1 | Historical Mode shows *Historical Mode needs host data access* |

The three layers are explained in [onboarding](../../operations/00-developer-onboarding.md);
`npm run doctor` reports which ones a machine has.

Warm-up and live ticks meet in the runtime; the archive is a separate path used
only by Historical Mode. A successful warm-up does not prove an archive
partition exists, and an archive answer says nothing about the live collector
today.

Live Mode also has a bounded `band_history` request for a historical price band.
The laptop sends the instrument, price range and time window through Terminal C;
the host replays its recorded raw depth and returns resting size and traded
flow per row. Institutional Order Blocks v1 reads a block the visible live
history did not cover from the host's stored heatmap columns first
(`/heatmap/columns`, cut to the block's rows in the browser) and uses
`band_history` only when the host has no stored columns for the pair or that
read fails. Session Levels reads a touched level's five minutes and a range's
totals from the same stored columns, with no raw-replay fallback. A missing book interval remains a gap, never a
zero-liquidity observation. Each host read is capped at two hours, 256 rows and
100,000 row/column cells; the client pieces longer requests. This is an
on-demand derivation, **not a stored canonical L2 row dataset**, and it does
not make order-book indicators available in the separate Historical Mode
archive. See the [wire contract](../../contracts/ws-frame-contract/v1/ws-frame-contract-v1.md)
and `apps/runtime/src/server/band-history.js` for the exact boundary.

## What each instrument has

| Instrument | Live ticks | Warm-up from the host | Historical candles | Historical studies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BTC, ETH spot and perpetual (Binance) | always | yes | 1m, 1h, 1d, each with taker buy volume | none yet |
| ES, NQ (continuous futures) | with layer 2 | yes, what the weekday collector captured | 1m, 1h, 1d | profile, VPIN, large prints, bar flow |
| AAPL, TSLA (equities) | with layer 2 | yes, what the weekday collector captured | 1m, 1h, 1d | profile, VPIN, large prints, bar flow |
| NVDA (equity) | with layer 2 | yes, what the weekday collector captured | not archived | none |

**Equity candles are regular session only**, live and historical alike:
09:30–16:00 New York time, with the exchange's holidays and early closes from
[`config/market-calendar.json`](../../../config/market-calendar.json). Hourly
bars start at 09:30, the last one ends at the bell, and a daily bar is that
session. Pre-market and after-hours prints are not in equity candles. Futures
and crypto candles are UTC-aligned and cover every traded minute.

Other timeframes are resampled in the browser from those rungs. The governed
instrument list, exact provider symbols and tick sizes live in
[`config/market-instruments.json`](../../../config/market-instruments.json);
the archive's list lives in `apps/web/src/lib/archive/historicalCatalog.ts`.

The four studies are computed on the host from trade-derived families, so the
browser never pages minute rows. Their request, limits and refusals are in the
[archive study contract](../../contracts/archive-study/v1/archive-study-v1.md).
In short: a profile per requested range, VPIN over whole UTC weeks (at most 26),

## Which indicators work in Historical Mode

`HISTORICAL_INDICATOR_POLICIES` in `apps/web/src/lib/chart/indicatorRegistry.ts`
decides this, per indicator, from the capabilities the selected archive
provides. There are three policies:

| Policy | Needs | Works on |
|---|---|---|
| Candles | `ohlcv` | every archived instrument |
| Candle flow | `ohlcv` + `buy_volume` | Binance (candles carry taker buy volume) and ES/NQ/AAPL/TSLA (the `bar_flow` study supplies both sides) |
| Trade families | `ohlcv` + `trade_families` | ES/NQ/AAPL/TSLA |

An indicator can also be marked unavailable with a reason, as order-book and
every-trade indicators are: the archive has no historical depth and keeps only
the largest prints of each minute.

## Rules every indicator follows

**Read buy and sell through `barSides`.** Never compute sell volume as
`volume - buy_volume`. `apps/web/src/lib/indicators/barSides.ts` returns
`{known, buy, sell, unknown, delta, sided}` for a bar. It uses an explicit
`sell_volume` when the bar has one, and falls back to `volume - buy_volume`
only for bars that carry just a buy side. That fallback is exact for Binance,
where every trade has a taker side. It is not exact for equities.

**Unsided volume is real and large for equities.** A Databento equity trade
often has no aggressor side. Measured on the host (Report 54): ES and NQ are
effectively fully sided, but AAPL is about 40% unsided on one-minute bars and
65–75% on hourly and daily bars; TSLA is 35–55%. Historical bars carry that
volume separately as `unknown`, and `unsidedShare(bars)` gives the share to
show beside an equity delta. Counting it as selling would make every equity
delta read bearish by that amount.

Live dxFeed bars built from the tape carry buy and sell only when every trade in
that minute had a side. A minute with any unsided trade carries neither, so
`barSides` reports it unknown and it draws no delta, which is common for
equities. Stored native dxFeed candles carry both sides explicitly (volume at
the ask and at the bid), with any remainder unknown.

**`missing` is not `empty`.** A study reports ranges it could not read as
`missing`; draw them as unavailable, never as zero. It reports ranges it read
and found no trades in (a weekend, an overnight hour for an equity) as `empty`,
and those legitimately have nothing to draw. A bar with no trades is absent
from `bar_flow`; draw no delta for it, not a zero delta.

**Gate on capabilities, not on provider names.** Declare what the indicator
needs through its `HISTORICAL_INDICATOR_POLICIES` entry. Do not branch on
`provider === "BINANCE"`. The map is typed over every indicator id, so a new
indicator does not compile until it has a policy.

**Warm-up failure is not an empty signal.** When the storage reader cannot
reach the host it reports the read as unavailable. Show the indicator as
warming up or degraded, never as a flat or zero reading.

**Quantities are provider units.** Futures sizes are contracts, equity sizes
are shares, Binance sizes are the base asset. Do not add them across providers
or convert them to a notional the provider did not report.

**Need a new trade-derived aggregate?** Do not fetch minute rows into the
browser. A new study is a change to the archive study contract and the host,
and goes through the owner.

## Adding an indicator that works in both modes

1. Create `apps/web/src/lib/indicators/<name>/`. The folder owns the whole
   indicator: computation, geometry, primitive and settings. Follow the
   [SDK execution model](../indicator-sdk/TA.01-indicator-authoring-core-evidence.md).
   Its settings dialog gets [setting presets](setting-presets.md): a
   `settingsSchema` is enough for the generic dialog, and a hand-built one
   declares a preset schema for every setting it stores.
2. Register it in `INDICATOR_REGISTRY` and give it a
   `HISTORICAL_INDICATOR_POLICIES` entry that names what it needs.
3. Read sides through `barSides`. For example, a cumulative delta that leaves
   bars without a known side undrawn:

   ```ts
   import type { BarFrame } from "../../../types/frames";
   import { barSides } from "../barSides";

   export function cumulativeDelta(bars: readonly BarFrame[]): (number | null)[] {
     let total = 0;
     return bars.map((bar) => {
       const sides = barSides(bar);
       if (!sides.known) return null;
       total += sides.delta;
       return total;
     });
   }
   ```

4. Check it in Live Mode on Binance (every machine can), then in Historical
   Mode on BTC and on one futures and one equity instrument (layer 1). On AAPL,
   confirm the unsided share is shown and the delta is not skewed.
5. Add tests for the computation, lifecycle, capability gating and rendering,
   as listed under *Adding or changing an indicator* in the
   [indicator index](README.md).

function bucketOpenMs(tsClose: number, intervalMs: number): number {
  return Math.floor((tsClose - 1) / intervalMs) * intervalMs;

function bucketFor(bar: BarFrame, intervalMs: number, sessionAnchored: boolean): { openMs: number; spanMs: number } {
  const session = sessionAnchored ? regularSessionAt(bar.ts_open) : null;
  if (session === null) return { openMs: bucketOpenMs(bar.ts_open + bar.interval_ms, intervalMs), spanMs: intervalMs };
  const openMs = session.openMs + Math.floor((bar.ts_open - session.openMs) / intervalMs) * intervalMs;
  return { openMs, spanMs: Math.min(intervalMs, session.closeMs - openMs) };
}

function isCompleteBucket(group: readonly BarFrame[], bucketOpen: number, targetIntervalMs: number): boolean {
  let expectedOpen = bucketOpen;
  for (const bar of group) {
    if (bar.ts_open !== expectedOpen || bar.closed !== true) return false;
    expectedOpen += bar.interval_ms;
  }
  return expectedOpen === bucketOpen + targetIntervalMs;
}

function isRepresentableBucket(group: readonly BarFrame[], bucketOpen: number): boolean {
  let expectedOpen = bucketOpen;
  for (let index = 0; index < group.length; index += 1) {
    const bar = group[index];
    if (bar.ts_open !== expectedOpen) return false;
    if (bar.closed !== true && index !== group.length - 1) return false;
    expectedOpen += bar.interval_ms;
  }
  return true;
}

function fieldKeyChanged<T>(
  field: SettingsFieldSchema<T>,
  oldSettings: T,
  newSettings: T,
): boolean {
  if (field.kind === "staticHint") return false;
  if (field.kind === "custom") {
    if (field.key === undefined) return false;
    return oldSettings[field.key] !== newSettings[field.key];
  }
  return oldSettings[field.key] !== newSettings[field.key];
}

The trading question is clear and useful, and the default view is appropriately focused. The calculation still has two repository-level mismatches and an incomplete replacement rule that need fixing before build approval.

1. **Specific trading question: passes.** “Is participation unusual for this time of day?” is one concrete question. The direction and follow-through disclaimer is appropriate. Describe the result as unusual *relative to recent comparable bars*; “lights up only when unusual” overstates what a short, noisy baseline can establish.

2. **Calculation: incremental and causal in principle, but not fully specified.**

   - **Short-bar normalization does not match the SDK.** The resampler calculates a shortened equity bucket’s session span, but emits `interval_ms: targetIntervalMs`. A 15:30–16:00 hourly candle therefore still declares 60 minutes. Dividing by `interval_ms` halves its actual volume rate. The early-close acceptance fixture assumes data the indicator will not receive. Derive scheduled duration using the existing market calendar, explicitly include that dependency in the data section, and test actual resampled `BarFrame`s. See [resampleBars.ts](/home/user/Terminal-L1-L2/Terminal-L1-L2-development-indicator-sdk/apps/web/src/lib/chart/resampleBars.ts:125).
   - **Retention is a bar count, not elapsed time.** `10_080 * interval_ms` is not the chart’s retention window. On one-minute equities, it discards observations after seven calendar days even though 10,080 regular-session bars cover about 25 sessions. Gaps and higher-timeframe resampling create further differences. Use the actual retained-series boundary through a supported runtime path, or explicitly choose a separate time-based horizon and remove the claim that it matches chart retention. See [candleLifecycle.ts](/home/user/Terminal-L1-L2/Terminal-L1-L2-development-indicator-sdk/apps/web/src/lib/chart/candleLifecycle.ts:3).
   - **Replacement handling must cover all derived state.** Updating the ring is insufficient: specify replacement of the rolling SMA’s last committed input and recomputation of the sustained counter from its value *before this bar*. Test a closed correction crossing `sustainedLevel` in both directions, including marker removal. Also specify valid→invalid and invalid→valid corrections.
   - **Fallback commitment conflicts with confirmation wording.** If an unclosed bar commits when the next bar arrives, its earlier output and marker remain provisional under the stated rendering rule. Define whether that is intentional; the runtime normally replaces only the current timestamp’s output.

   Read-before-write medians and full-bar expectations for forming bars otherwise avoid future data.

3. **Data availability: mostly passes, with the duration exception above.** Total volume requires no sided-volume inference, and `OHLCV_POLICY` is appropriate. Invalid volume and gaps are handled honestly. Resolve the contradiction between calculation step 1’s mandatory crypto weekday/weekend families and the advanced setting’s default `weekendSplit: false`. Write one explicit clock/family resolution rule.

4. **Default usability: passes with small corrections.** One ratio, a 1× reference, orange unusual bars, and three primary controls are understandable quickly. Specify complete status precedence: unsupported timeframe → invalid volume → insufficient samples → unusable baseline → forming → ready. Otherwise a forming, unready bar could misleadingly display only “versus full-bar expectation.” Label the table sample count as prior samples used in the denominator, excluding the current bar.

5. **Validation: testable in outline, but the baseline and replay claims need tightening.**

   - The calculation alternates between rolling **volumes** and rolling **rates**. Choose rates for both methods when durations differ; one-minute fixtures conceal this inconsistency.
   - Replace “REBUILT reproduces live state exactly” with equivalence on bars whose complete dependencies are retained. Earlier retained outputs can legitimately lose their pre-retention warm-up. Sustained markers also depend on run history, so define their comparison eligibility separately or provide a bounded reset rule.
   - The weekday readiness expectation is wrong for arbitrary seven-day start dates: three prior weekdays need not exist by calendar day 4. Freeze the start timestamp and assert readiness from actual prior family observations.
   - Freeze fixture generation order, anomaly sampling/deduplication, and the extra 1,920 rollover bars. A seed alone does not specify these.
   - The same-bar, same-threshold comparison is fair for the stated descriptive experiment. However, the equity fixture deliberately contains clock seasonality and places every anomaly at midday. Add a flat-seasonality control and anomalies at the open/close before making broader claims about superiority.

6. **Simplest alternative:** ship the same-slot median ratio with one line, the readiness table, lookback and threshold controls. Keep rolling RVOL in the validation report; defer sustained markers and optional weekend splitting. That retains most of the trading value while removing substantial state and UX complexity.
