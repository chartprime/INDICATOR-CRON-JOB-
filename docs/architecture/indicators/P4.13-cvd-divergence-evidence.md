# CVD Divergence (P4.13)

> **Historical implementation evidence.** This dated page records the result
> of a completed build. It is not a current deployment/health statement; use
> the [final-state indicator index](README.md) and the handbook for supported
> operation.

CVD Divergence is a **research annotation**. It highlights a bar where a
confirmed pivot in a rolling delta oscillator is not confirmed by price. It is
not a reversal forecast, an entry/exit instruction, or a claim about
participant intent.

It is registered as `cvdDivergence` (`kind: "runtime"`, category `l1`), ported
from a community Pine study ("Cumulative Volume Delta Divergence").

## Relationship to Delta Divergence (P4.03)

Both draw divergence annotations and both are research annotations, but they
invert one another and therefore surface different bars. Neither supersedes the
other; both stay registered.

|                  | Delta Divergence                  | CVD Divergence                                |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Pivots found on  | **price** closes                  | the **oscillator**, or price closes by option |
| Compared against | the oscillator                    | **price** closes                              |
| Oscillator       | unbounded cumulative delta        | **rolling** delta sum                         |
| Oscillator pane  | shared with Aggressive Order Flow | its own                                       |

## Product contract

Five behavioural controls:

- **CVD period** (1–200, default 21) — the rolling window length (the study's
  `Period`).
- **Divergence fractal periods** (1–20, default 5) — bars required on each side
  of an oscillator pivot (the study's `n`, which the study set to 2).
- **Detect pivots on price** (default off) — swaps the series the pivots are
  found on, described under "Pivoting on price" below.
- **Min bars between pivots** (1–100, default 5) — the closest two pivots may
  be and still count as a pair.
- **Max bars between pivots** (1–100, default 50) — the furthest apart.

Plus **Show divergence labels** (visibility only), plus the borrowed-series
controls described under "Borrowed cumulative CVD overlay" below.

The default **fractal width of 5** is the operator's own setting, widened from
the study's 2. It has a second consequence worth stating: the fractal rule is
what guarantees two same-kind pivots cannot be closer than `2 * pivotPeriods +
1` bars, so at 5 that floor rises to **11** bars. A wider fractal therefore
rejects short-wiggle pairs by construction, before the distance window below is
consulted at all.

The default **maximum of 50** is likewise the operator's own setting; it
descends from the source study's 30-bar proximity window but is deliberately
wider.

The default **minimum of 5** is deliberately dwarfed by that fractal floor. The
fractal rule already guarantees two same-kind pivots are at least
`2 * pivotPeriods + 1` bars apart, which at the default `pivotPeriods` of 5 is
11 — so at the defaults the minimum of 5 adds no rejection the geometry did not
already impose. It only binds when `pivotPeriods` is lowered (1 makes the
fractal floor 3), which is why the fixtures used by the unit tests place their
pivots 6 bars apart while passing an explicit distance range: a 6-bar pair
clears the fractal floor at `pivotPeriods` 1 with room to spare, so a test that
means to exercise the divergence rule rather than the distance filter has to
state the window it wants instead of inheriting one.

`normalizedPivotDistance` clamps both bounds to their own limits and orders
them before the detector sees them. The detector throws on an inverted range,
which is correct for a literal caller — but an operator can drag the minimum
past the current maximum on the way to correcting the other field, and that
throw would abort the chart render.

There is no confidence threshold, statistical population gate, or hidden signal
score. A divergence satisfying the rules below is drawn rather than suppressed
by an opaque ranking model.

## Deterministic methodology

1. Only `closed: true` bars participate. An unfinished live bar cannot confirm
   a pivot.
2. Per-bar delta is `buy_volume - sell_volume` through `barSides`, falling back
   to `volume - buy_volume` when a bar carries no explicit sell volume. This is
   the same measurement Aggressive Order Flow's CVD uses, so the two indicators
   cannot disagree about what traded.
3. The oscillator is a **rolling** sum of the most recent `CVD period` deltas
   (`math.sum(delta, Period)`), not a cumulative total. Rolling is what makes
   the oscillator mean-revert around zero, which is what makes its own pivots
   meaningful.
4. An oscillator pivot high/low is a **strict** extreme over `pivotPeriods`
   completed bars on both sides. An equal neighbour fails the test, so a
   plateau never pivots.
5. Consecutive confirmed pivots of the same kind form a candidate pair. The
   pair is rejected when its bar distance falls outside the configured range.
6. Both pivot values must sit on the side of zero the pattern is about:
   below zero for bullish, above zero for bearish. Without this gate two
   oscillator highs that both sit below zero against a rising price would be
   reported as a bearish divergence — a line under the baseline reading as
   noise rather than distribution. This reproduces the source study's own
   `Low_Last_Hist < 0` / `High_Last_Hist > 0` gates.
7. **Bearish:** price closes higher AND the oscillator prints a lower-or-equal
   high. **Bullish:** price closes lower AND the oscillator prints a
   higher-or-equal low.
8. A candidate is unavailable, not neutral, when any bar between the two
   pivots has an unknown side split or a real data hole: bars of another
   interval, time running backwards, more uncovered time than the venue's
   ordinary close, or an intraday equity gap over regular-session hours. A
   scheduled close (overnight, weekend) is not a hole, so oscillator pivots
   on either side of it still pair. Divergences on the borrowed cumulative
   series break at every `ts_open` gap instead, because `computeCVDForBars`
   restarts its running total there and two pivots across that restart
   belong to different totals.

Steps 4 through 7 read the series the pivots were found on. Swapping that
series swaps which side of each comparison moves, as described next.

### Pivoting on price

**Detect pivots on price** turns the detector around, so the pivots come from
the price close and the CVD is read only at those two bars:

- Pivot highs and lows are strict extremes of the **price** close over
  `pivotPeriods` bars on both sides, under the same fractal rule as above.
  A bar where the CVD is null or restarts (a hole; for the cumulative CVD,
  any gap) counts as missing, exactly as it does for the CVD's own pivots, so
  no pivot's look-around reaches across one into another stretch of data.
- The CVD takes no part in choosing the pivots. It need not have a pivot
  anywhere near them, and it is sampled at the pivot bars only.
- The zero-side gate (step 6) is dropped. In this direction the sign of the
  CVD says nothing about the pattern, so requiring the first pivot above zero
  and the second below would reject the lines the mode exists to draw.
- **Bearish:** price closes higher AND the CVD at those bars is
  lower-or-equal. **Bullish:** price closes lower AND the CVD is
  higher-or-equal.
- A pair is unavailable when the CVD has no value at either pivot bar, or
  when a hole or an unknown side split falls between the two pivots, exactly
  as in step 8.
- The pivot-distance range and the per-series trend filter keep applying, the
  filter being read at the confirming bar in both modes.

The control is one switch for both series: it moves the oscillator pivots and
the borrowed cumulative pivots together, since the two panes are read the same
way at the same time.

## Column shading

Each histogram column is shaded by its momentum against the three preceding
columns (the study's own comparison, stated by the operator):

| Column     | Condition                                   | Tone          |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Above zero | stronger than the average of the previous 3 | intense green |
| Above zero | weaker                                      | pale green    |
| Below zero | weaker than that average                    | intense red   |
| Below zero | stronger (i.e. recovering toward zero)      | pale red      |

"Stronger" means **more positive** in both halves, so a deeply negative column
recovering toward zero reads as strengthening and takes the pale red. An exact
tie is not stronger. A column whose comparison is not yet defined — warm-up, or
a window spanning a gap — falls back to intense by sign rather than vanishing,
because a column with a real value must never silently disappear.

## Rendering contract

- The oscillator is drawn **uncompressed**, in its own pane, so a column's
  height equals the rolling sum itself. (`toHistogramData` would compress
  outliers; `toTonedHistogramData` is used with compression disabled.)
- Divergence segments are drawn in the oscillator pane between the two pivot
  points, reusing `DeltaDivergencePrimitive("cvd")` with an explicit line
  palette so segments and columns share one colour pair.
- `DiV+` (bullish) and `DiV-` (bearish) are drawn on the **price** pane as text
  plates — white glyphs on a filled coloured rectangle.
- Divergences found on the borrowed cumulative series use the **same geometry**
  (the same confirming-bar anchoring, offset and directional arrow) but a
  different mark: a bare `⯁` glyph in the same directional colour, with **no
  plate behind it** — colour on transparent rather than white on colour. The
  glyph replaces the words entirely, because the two series annotate different
  things (a mean-reverting rolling sum and a drifting cumulative total) and both
  can sit on the chart at once; a shape is the one thing that cannot be misread
  as `DiV+` / `DiV-` at a glance. Only the glyph and the plate are per-style,
  which is why the two markers cannot drift apart.
- Each label is a plate with a **directional arrow**: bullish sits below the
  bar with an up arrow, bearish above it with a down arrow, so direction is
  readable by shape and not only by colour. The cumulative diamond keeps that
  arrow, so its direction stays readable without the words.
- Labels anchor to the **wick extreme** of the confirming bar (`low` for
  bullish, `high` for bearish) even though **detection compares closes**. A
  marker drawn at the close would sit inside the candle body. The shared
  `Divergence` type carries only closes, so the bars are supplied alongside the
  divergences in a single call, and a missing index is skipped rather than
  thrown — a live bar being appended can briefly leave the two arrays one bar
  apart.
- Labels are anchored to the **confirming** bar (`priceTo`), the moment the
  pattern became knowable, never the earlier pivot, so nothing repaints.

## Borrowed cumulative CVD overlay

**Overlay cumulative CVD** (off by default) borrows Aggressive Order Flow's
cumulative running total into this indicator's own pane as a reference, so a
trader can see absolute flow alongside the rolling oscillator without enabling
a second indicator. **Cumulative CVD display** (line, default) chooses between
a line and 1-minute-derived candles, matching that indicator's own control.

Both forms are computed by calling the _same_
`computeDeltaPerBar` / `computeCVDForBars` / `computeCvdCandles` the other
indicator uses, so the two renderings cannot disagree about the shape.

Two deliberate choices:

- **Its own price scale.** The cumulative total runs to tens of thousands while
  the rolling oscillator stays near plus/minus (period x bar delta). Sharing
  one scale would flatten the oscillator's columns into a line, so the borrowed
  series gets `cvd-divergence-cumulative` -- the same separation Aggressive
  Order Flow already gives its histogram.
- **Off by default.** CVD Divergence's own reading is the point of the
  indicator; the cumulative reference is opt-in.

The candle form needs 1-minute source bars (`applyRawBars`). Without them
nothing is drawn, which is honest rather than synthesising an intrabar path
from a coarser interval. This mirrors the same requirement and the same
behaviour in Aggressive Order Flow.

This is a one-way borrow of a _computation_, not a cross-indicator attachment:
the series are owned and paced entirely by this indicator. It therefore does
not depend on `RuntimeIndicatorDef.crossAttachments`, which
[remains unimplemented](P4.03-delta-divergence-evidence.md).

### Divergences on the borrowed series

**Divergences on cumulative CVD** (off by default) runs the SAME detector over
the cumulative series and marks its results on the price pane as a `⯁` diamond —
the oscillator's geometry, offset and directional arrow, but a bare
directional-coloured glyph with no plate and no words. The two families are
therefore distinguishable at a glance when both are on the chart.

That toggle is **independent of `showCumulativeCvd`**, and deliberately so: the
line is a reference overlay and the divergences are a signal, and wanting either
without the other is legitimate. Critically, the toggle gates only the
**drawing** — detection always runs while the indicator is on. An earlier
revision tied detection to the overlay being drawn, which silently starved the
markers of data whenever the line was hidden; keeping detection and rendering on
separate switches is what makes that class of bug impossible here.

Its pivots use their own **min/max bars between cum. CVD pivots** (defaults 1
and 60), because a drifting line turns far less often than a mean-reverting
rolling sum: one shared window would either miss its pivots or admit oscillator
noise.

The **zero-side gate is skipped** for this series, and that is the one place the
two detections genuinely differ. The gate exists because the rolling
oscillator's sign is meaningful — two positive highs against a rising price are
not distribution, just an oscillator that never went negative. A cumulative
total drifts and can stay positive for days, so requiring both pivots below
zero would suppress almost every bullish divergence on it. The gate therefore
lives in `DivergenceDetectionOptions.requireZeroSide`, ON for the oscillator
and OFF here, rather than being hard-coded in the shared scanner.

The segments follow whichever cumulative series is actually plotted. When the
cumulative series is switched to candles, the line is hidden, and a primitive
attached to a hidden series still asks its host for `priceToCoordinate` — which
in candle mode resolves against the Candlestick's scale, not the line's. The
segments are re-hosted onto the visible series for that reason.

Both marker families are chart-owned primitives on the **main candle series**,
so both live and die with the chart: every one of their refs must be cleared in
the chart-destroy cleanup, not just the series refs. The cumulative labels and
their segment host were the pair left out, and because StrictMode runs the
chart effect twice on mount, the stale ref made the `if (!ref)` guard skip
re-attaching — leaving the diamonds invisible for the life of the session. That
is the same failure class the oscillator's own labels had, and the cleanup now
clears both for the same reason.

### Trend filters

Each series has its **own independent** trend filter, with its own toggle, its
own moving-average length and its own **Inverse** switch: **Filter oscillator**
and **Filter cumulative**. Both filters default to off. One shared toggle was
deliberately avoided — the two series have different shapes, so a window that
suits the mean-reverting oscillator would not suit the drifting cumulative
line, and switching one on silently switching the other on would be a surprise.
The direction switch is separate for the same reason: the two series are not
expected to want the same reading, so one shared direction setting would force
a compromise on one of them.

By default, a bullish divergence is admitted only while its series sits
**above** its average and a bearish one only while it sits **below**. The test is
strict, so sitting exactly ON the average is not a trend in either direction.

**Inverse** reverses that pairing: bullish below, bearish above.

```
              bullish      bearish
normal        above avg    below avg
inverse       below avg    above avg
```

The cumulative series' switch **defaults to on**, because a drifting running
total most often confirms a bullish divergence while it is still _below_ its
own average — the normal reading selects the rarer population of bars there.
The oscillator's defaults to off. Because the switches are independent, this is
not a global flip: each series can be read either way, and both default values
can be changed independently of one another.

Two things inversion does **not** change, and both matter:

- A bar whose average is not yet defined — still warming up, or after a gap —
  is admitted in **both** directions either way. Inverting cannot alter what
  the first bars of a chart show.
- An exact tie is still admitted by neither direction. Inverting mirrors the
  comparison, it does not make the filter more permissive.

The `Inverse` switch stays live even while its filter's toggle is off: it is
only _read_ while the filter is enabled, but disabling it would make the
setting unreachable exactly when an operator wants to prepare it. The switch is
carried on the detection call as `DivergenceDetectionOptions.trendInverted`,
per call, so the two series never share one direction by accident.

Three details worth stating because each is a deliberate choice:

- The average is computed through the shared `ta` library's own `sma`
  (`smaForSeries`), not a second implementation, so it is the same average the
  terminal's other SMAs draw. `ta.sma` is stream-based and refuses to run
  outside a creation window, so the array replay opens that window explicitly —
  which is what `createState` does for an indicator's own streams.
- A `null` in the source series **restarts** the average rather than being
  skipped. `ta.sma` would otherwise average across a gap, inventing a value
  spanning two unrelated CVD epochs — the same boundary the oscillator itself
  restarts on.
- The filter is applied at the **confirming** pivot (`priceTo`), the bar the
  trader acts on, not the earlier pivot. A bar whose average is still warming up
  is **admitted** rather than suppressed: dropping signals until the average
  fills would silently shorten the start of every chart instead of filtering it
  by trend.

The average is also drawn as a hairline overlay on the pane of the series it
gates, so the trend being filtered on is visible rather than implied.

## Provider provenance and limits

The oscillator is provider-neutral only after the common `BarFrame` boundary.
Its measurement is provider-specific, exactly as documented for
[P4.02 Aggressive Order Flow](P4.02-aggressive-order-flow-evidence.md):

- **Binance:** `buy_volume` is exchange-supplied kline taker-buy base volume.
- **dxFeed:** `buy_volume` is constructed from eligible, terminal-classified
  TimeAndSale prints.

A Binance reading and a dxFeed reading must not be treated as directly
interchangeable. Where a bar carries no side split at all — dxFeed futures bars
may not — the oscillator is **unavailable** for the windows spanning it, not
zero.

Historical availability follows the shared candle-flow policy
(`CANDLE_FLOW_POLICY`: `ohlcv` + `buy_volume`), so the indicator
appears in Historical Mode only where the archive supplies a per-bar side
split. Registration is not evidence that archive inputs exist.

## Deliberate departures from the source study

Removed, with the operator's agreement, as unused or out of scope:

- an EMA(50) trend filter,
- ATR(55),
- an unused `UltraData` input,
- the 30-bar proximity **hard-code** (now the configurable maximum, which
  defaults to 50),
- the `Bear_Phase`/`Bull_Phase` histogram scan,
- the "Normal/Good/Strong" divergence counting and its labels,
- the study's own `+RD`/`-RD` wording (renamed `DiV+`/`DiV-` by request).

The study's `Buying`/`Selling` price-position estimate
(`volume * (close - low) / (high - low)`) was implemented, then **deliberately
removed** at the operator's direction in favour of the terminal's own taker
volume, so this indicator and Aggressive Order Flow agree by construction.
