## Level calculation

Standard convention, matching TradingView: **level 0% sits at the
second-clicked point's price — the end of the measured move — and level 100%
at the first-clicked point's price**, every level in between a direct linear
interpolation —
`price = pointB.price + fraction * (pointA.price - pointB.price)`.

A retracement level answers "how far has price pulled back from the end of
this move toward where it began". Drawn bottom-up on a rally from 100 to 200,
the 0% line sits at the high of 200 and the 61.8% line at **138.2** — 61.8% of
the way back down. It works identically in either drag direction, since the
fraction is interpolated between whichever two prices were actually clicked
rather than a fixed "high anchor"/"low anchor".

The 7 levels: 0%, 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%, 100% — no
extension levels, a confirmed scope decision (nothing beyond the standard
retracement set was wanted).

> **Corrected 2026-09-02 (`L1L2-V2-088`).** This section previously described
> the inverse — 0% at the *first* point — while still claiming to match
> TradingView, and the implementation and its tests both followed the doc. The
> levels rendered upside down, reported externally during review. Anchoring is
> now the standard one and is pinned by tests naming the expected prices.

There is nothing user-configurable about the level set, so unlike Trendline
this indicator has no settings modal.

## Reuse, not duplication

`fibonacciGeometry.ts` imports `PixelSegment`/`hitTestTrendlines` directly
from `trendlineGeometry.ts` rather than re-implementing point-to-segment
hit-testing — that function was already fully generic and never referenced
anything trendline-specific. A retracement's 7 level lines become 7 hit
candidates sharing one drawing id, so a click near *any* level selects the
whole retracement, not an individual line. `advanceDrawingPlacement` (the
placement state machine) is reused completely unchanged.

## The real generalization this ticket required

`TrendlinePrimitive.hitTestDrawing` used to return just the winning id.
Once a second primitive exists, correctly resolving a click against
whichever drawing is actually closer requires comparing a hit from each
primitive by distance, not just checking one before the other.
`hitTestTrendlines` now returns `{ id, distance } | null`, both primitives'
`hitTestDrawing` match, and a new `pickNearestHit` helper does the
cross-primitive comparison — unit-tested directly rather than left as
untested inline logic in `ChartPanel.tsx`. `ChartPanel.tsx` also gained
`syncDrawingPrimitives`/`setSelectedDrawing`/`setPendingOnActiveTool`,
three small helpers that route each add/select/delete/preview operation to
whichever primitive (or both) actually needs it, replacing what would
otherwise have been three duplicated filter-and-dispatch call sites — this
is exactly the one-line-per-call-site adaptation the P4.04 evidence doc
predicted would be needed here, now real and tested rather than
hypothetical.
