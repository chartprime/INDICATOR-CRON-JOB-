# Live Range Control

A card over a range of the chart: what traded there, one delta, the big prints by side, the minutes each side won, the price each side moved, and a verdict on who moved the range. Indicator id `liveRangeControl`, category L2, not offered on historical charts. Browser only: it reads the candles the chart holds and the heatmap's live columns, asks the host, through the column history route the heatmap already uses, for the hours the runtime does not hold, and adds no request or frame of its own. In the two zone modes it also draws the zone on the chart: two vertical lines with a soft shade between them, which the trader drags on the chart itself.

## The three modes

The Mode setting picks the range (`LIVE_RANGE_MODES`, `rangeModeOf`):

- **Visible range** (the default): the candles on screen, from the open of the first candle in the chart's visible logical range to the end of the last one, never past now (`visibleSpan`, the logical range rounded to whole candles, the candle length from the chart's last two candles, `DEFAULT_CANDLE_MS` for a single one). Scrolling or zooming moves it; the card reads again once a second while shown (`LIVE_TICK_MS`) and shortly after the chart repaints: a repaint only asks for a read, which follows 120 ms later, once for all the repaints in between (`READ_SETTLE_MS`), so a scroll costs the chart nothing. Nothing is drawn and every row of every column counts.
- **Selected zone**: a fixed window between two handles, start and end, drawn as two lines with a soft shade between them.
- **Live**: from the start handle until now, one handle and one line; the shade grows with the clock.

The window lives on a slider over the last seven days in whole minutes, as far back as the host keeps its ten-second columns (the pyramid's retention, `DEFAULT_RETENTION_MS`): minute 0 is seven days ago on the whole minute (`originOf`, `WINDOW_SPAN_MS`), the far end is now, and the handles stay a minute apart (`windowBounds`, `windowToMinutes`, `windowFromMinutes`). A handle at its end means "seven days ago" or "now" and follows the clock; a handle moved holds its clock time, so a Live start set at 14:00 stays at 14:00 while the seven days move on, until it falls out of them and rests at their edge. Over 10,080 minutes a step of the slider is about half an hour; the lines on the chart move by the minute. Live drops a fixed end (`windowFor`). The window is one for the whole terminal, not per instrument: a switch of instrument keeps it, and it is kept across a reload under `terminal.live-range-control.window.v1` (`loadWindow`, `saveWindow`; a broken entry reads as the whole window). The dialog ticks once a second while open in a zone mode, so its label and handles follow the clock.

The card tells only when what it shows would change (`reportSignature`). Its second line names the range: "02:35 → 03:05 UTC · 30 min" for the screen or a fixed window, "since 02:35 UTC · 30 min" while a zone runs live, the first day added when the range crosses midnight, hours from two hours, days from two days (`rangeLine`, `spanLabel`, `sinceLabel`, `spanText`).

## The zone

In the two zone modes the window is drawn on the chart: a soft shade in the zone colour over the whole height of the price pane, from the start to the end of the window, or to now in Live, under the candles, and a vertical line in the zone colour on each handle above them, none at now in Live (`ShadeRenderer`, `LineRenderer`, `SHADE_ALPHA`). A moment sits inside its candle by the share of the candle that has run, so a start at 14:20 on an hourly chart is a third into the 14:00 candle; past the last candle and before the first the place runs on at the length of a candle, and a moment in a gap between candles sits at the right edge of the candle before it (`logicalOfTime`, `candleMsOf`). With Show zone off nothing is drawn and no line can be dragged, while the card goes on reading the window (`zoneShownOf`).

The lines move on the chart itself, the way the order book profile's reach handles do. Over a line, within 6 px of it on the price pane (`LINE_HIT_PX`, `lineAt`), the chart shows a resize cursor (`hitTest`) and the line thickens; a press there takes the pointer from the chart, so the chart does not scroll, and each move puts the line on the whole minute under the pointer, read back from the chart's position (`timeOfLogical`, `dragTo`). The chart places only whole candles, so a place between two is worked out from the spacing of two whole candles, both ways (`xOfLogical`, `logicalOfX`). The two lines stay a minute apart and push each other along; the end line dragged to now follows the clock again, and the start line dragged past the seven days rests at their edge. A move only repaints the lines; the card reads on the release (`endDrag`) and, as always, once a second, and the window goes to MainPanel, which keeps it and hands it to the dialog, so the lines and the dialog's handles are the same window. A press with another button, while a drawing tool is on, or that another handle took first, stays with the chart.

There is no price band: the zone is a stretch of time, and the card reads every trade and big print in it, the same way the visible range reads the screen. Without candles the card still reads its window from the columns; the lines wait for the candles.

## The card

The Candle Monitor's size at Medium, 0.85 of it at Small and 1.25 at Large (`cardSizeOf`; one factor, `--lrc-k`, scales its width, padding, columns and every font in it), in the top-right column under it (a placed card fits itself to the pane again when its size changes; `cardPosition.ts` keeps a dragged spot under `terminal.live-range-control.card-position.v1`, a double-click puts it back; a press under 3 px is a click, `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX`). The title, "on screen", "selected zone" or "live zone" beside it (`headText`), the range on its own line, then up to five rows, buyers against sellers, with no sub-lines (the span is on the line above and the README has the rest). Each row can be switched off in the settings (`ROW_SETTINGS`, `rowShownOf`); the rows left keep their order, and with none left the card draws no table, only its head, span, verdict and note:

- **Traded volume**: the buys and the sells that traded in the range, from the runtime's one-second columns for the last twenty minutes, its ten-second columns for the hours before, and the host's ten-second columns before that (`readLiveRange`, `sumColumn`). A column's trades count in proportion to the part of it inside the range; the ten-second tier is read only up to the seam where a ten-second window ends inside the one-second tier, the one-second tier from there (`seamOf`), so no second is counted twice.
- **Delta**: one signed cell across both columns, buys − sells of all the traded volume ("+1.2k"), in the bid colour above zero, the ask colour below, neither at nothing (`toneOf`). Big prints are not taken out of it. The z is the net over the root of the summed squares of the range's minute nets (`noise` on the read, `NOISE_BUCKET_MS`; a column's net goes to the minute of its middle, weighted like its sums). It is the one tape reading with one scale on every instrument: measured over a week of BTC perp and NQ (`Trading indicators/lrc-ledger-measurement/range-results.md`), from 1.5 it named a side in 13–26 % of the one-hour to four-hour ranges and agreed with the range's own move 86–96 % of the time. The card calls a side at the verdict sensitivity, from 1 by default (the trader's choice, below); the week's median |z| was 0.79–1.00, so from 1 a little under half the ranges get a side, a share the measurement did not score.
- **Big prints**: the volume of the prints from the slider's size up, by side, and their count in brackets on a line under it, smaller and not bold, so a low slider never pushes the cell past its column ("34" over "(5)"; a count from 10,000 on in k and M, "(12k)", `printCountText`), from the prints the runtime keeps on each column (`readPrints`; the card reads them through a running `PrintLedger`, below). A print counts once however many columns carry it (by its id), at the first column read, and only inside the window. The runtime keeps every print from a rolling floor per column, at most 64, so a slider under that floor reads only the prints that were kept.
- **Minutes won**: of the range's clock minutes, how many buyers won (net buys) and how many sellers (net sells), "18 min" against "34 min" (`minutesWon` on the read, the same minute nets the z is made of). A minute with no trades or an even minute goes to nobody. It is the texture behind the z: a big z with few minutes won means one burst did it.
- **Price impact**: how much price each side moved its way in the minutes it won, in price points, and under it the points per thousand of that side's volume ("+12" over "(1.9/k)" under Buyers; "−48" over "(7.5/k)" under Sellers, down being the sellers' way). Each clock minute of the range gets a price, the volume-weighted price of its trades (every column's traded rows times the row width, the host's columns in their own row width), and the side that won the minute (net buys or net sells) is credited with the move from the previous counted minute's price to this one's, along with its own volume in that minute; the first counted minute, an even minute and a minute whose volume is under a tenth of the range's average minute (`IMPACT_MIN_SHARE`) move nothing for anyone, the quiet minute so that a few lots through an empty book do not read as impact. A side with no minute won shows a dash. The points are rounded like a size, one decimal under ten, whole to a thousand, k and M from there, and the under-line is the points per thousand units of the side, rounded the same (`pointsText`). Buyers with more volume but less impact than sellers is the reading this row exists for: the volume row says who traded more, this one says whose trading moved price. It is tape and traded prices only, no book.

With Show hover tips on (the default, `tipsShownOf`), hovering either price impact cell shows a tip under the pointer that says it in words (`impactTip`): how far price went in the minutes that side won ("Price went up 12 points in the minutes buyers won"), on how much of its size ("while buyers bought 6.5k BTC", the unit of the instrument), per thousand of it ("1.9 points up for every 1,000 BTC bought"), and one line that reads it against the other side: absorbed when price went against the side, "only buyers moved price their way" when the other side did not, else which side moved price further per unit, or about the same inside 1.25× of each other (`IMPACT_EVEN_RATIO`). The two cells take the pointer (`.live-range-hover`, a help cursor) while the rest of the card lets it through to the chart; with the tips off, or the row hidden, they let it through too, and a tip shown at that moment goes and stays gone until the pointer moves over a cell again. The tip spans the card and sits 14 px under the pointer (`TIP_GAP_PX`), or as far above it when less than 110 px of the pane is left below (`TIP_ROOM_PX`). It follows the card's numbers while it is shown and goes when the pointer leaves the cell or the card loses its rows.

Above the table, between the range line and the rows, one line says who moved the range, from the tape alone, bigger and bolder than the rows so it reads first, with the z beside it in the same colour (`.live-range-verdict`, `.live-range-verdict-z`; Balanced keeps the plain colour), the only place the z shows: at the verdict sensitivity of the settings (`zFromOf`, `VERDICT_Z_FROM`): at Normal, the default, from z +1 "Buyers in control" in the bid colour, from −1 "Sellers in control" in the ask colour, "Balanced" between; at Strict the same from ±2; at High a side is named as soon as the z leaves 0, Balanced only at a z of 0 (`verdictOf`); the z beside it (`zText`). Two states come before the z, in the plain colour and with no z: "Not enough data" while half or more of the range's candle time has no data (`VERDICT_HELD_FROM`, the last line below says which part), and "Too few minutes" while buyers and sellers won fewer than five minutes between them (`VERDICT_MIN_MINUTES`). The z of a single minute is always ±1, and the z of n minutes is at most √n, so under five minutes the z says more about the count of minutes than about the tape, and Strict could never name a side under four. The primitive gates its reports on the verdict at that sensitivity too (`reportSignature`), so a sensitivity that changes the verdict reports at once and one that does not stays quiet; nothing while the range has no trades. While any hour the range asked the host for is still out (`loading` on the report, `LiveRangeHistory.covered`), the card holds the verdict and its z back and shows a small turning ring with the word loading in the verdict's place above the table, instead of a share of missing data that is only the hours not yet landed; the note and the verdict come when the hours are in. It describes the range, never the next move: over the week nothing, tape or levels, predicted the move after a range (40–55 %).

The last line notes what is missing: waiting for the candles or for the zone's first candle, or no trades on screen or in the zone yet. While the hours of the range are still coming from the host it shows no note, and the spinner stands above the table in the verdict's place. Once they are in, it says which part of the range neither the runtime nor the host recorded, from a tenth of the range on (`MISSING_SAID_FROM`, `missingText`): "No data for 14:10–15:00 UTC · 83 % of the range" for one stretch, "No data for 1.3 h in 3 stretches · 40 % of the range" for several, the first day added when a stretch crosses midnight. The reader works the stretches out (`missing` and `tradedMs` on the read): the stretches the runtime holds no column for (`gridGaps`), less the host's columns in the grid's row width, inside the time the chart has candles (`tradedSpans`), so a closed market, the nightly break or the weekend, is never missing; nothing past the runtime's newest column, and nothing while the runtime holds no column at all. The share is of the candles' time in the range. On 4h and 1D candles the nightly break of an index future lies inside a candle and counts, about 4 to 8 % over a week, under the tenth. From half of it the verdict says Not enough data. The range ends at this browser's clock, and the seconds past the runtime's newest column are read when that column arrives.

## Resolution and sources

The prints are read through a ledger of their own (`PrintLedger`): it keeps the line-up of columns the reader walks (host, ten-second, one-second), owns each print's id to the first column that carries it, and tallies each column's prints at the slider's size once; a read adds the tallies of the columns inside the window and scans only the columns at its edges and the few carrying a print an earlier column owns, so it reads what `readPrints` reads in 3.5 ms over seven days of columns where the scan takes 84 ms. New columns at the end and a changed last column replay from there; a new slider size redoes the tallies only (16 ms over seven days); a line-up whose first column is another starts over. Measured on seven synthetic days (60,360 host columns): the whole settled read, trades and prints together, takes 25 ms, and 2 ms over one day; the first prints ledger build takes 120 ms, once. A week of host columns comes as 118 MB of JSON in 168 requests, of which the browser keeps the trade rows and the prints.

The hours before the runtime's oldest column come from the host, the way IOB v1 fills its zone windows (`liveRangeHistory`): the layer gets its own lane of the chart's column history stream (`forkForLiveRange`, a five-second pause after a failed page where the heatmap's lanes wait thirty, `HISTORY_RETRY_MS`) and asks for the stretches of the range the runtime holds no column for (`gridGaps`): the time before its oldest column, and any hole of a minute or more (`GAP_MIN_MS`) between its columns, as after a collector outage or a book stall, up to its newest column; the live edge past that column is the runtime's alone. The host is asked from the first stretch to the end of the last in hour chunks: newest first for the hours on screen, oldest first for a zone (`want(..., oldestFirst)`), so a zone reaching back days fills in time order. Of the host's columns, the reader counts the part of each that lies inside a stretch, so no second is counted twice, and the prints ledger lines up only those that lie whole inside one, in time order with the runtime's columns. Every page is kept and counted the moment it lands, so a range before the runtime's columns fills page by page. A zone asks oldest first because the prints ledger replays from the first changed column: an hour landing in front of all the others would start it over (about 200 ms over a week), while hours landing in time order cost it a few milliseconds each. The layer says it changed on every page that brought columns and again when an hour completes, so the card learns the hours are in without waiting for its next tick; when a later page of an hour fails, what landed stays and the columns already held are not counted twice. The layer keeps each host column's trade rows and nothing of its book (`HostColumn`, `hostColumnOf`: its time, row width, buys and sells by row, its big prints and the floor they were kept from). The host is asked only for hours the layer never held; an hour held only to the edge of the runtime's columns is asked again once the range reaches more than a minute past it (`STALE_CHUNK_MS`); a switch of instrument starts over. The layer lets go of the hours that lie wholly outside the range and as much again on each side, an hour at least (`keep`, `KEEP_PAD_MIN_MS`), so a zone dragged across the week holds its range and its margins rather than the week; a dropped hour is asked again once the range reaches it, and nothing is let go while a page is being folded in. Measured in Edge on the BTC perpetual (2026-10-13 16:40 to 10-20 16:00 UTC, 53,480 host columns, 5.5 trade rows and 4.8 prints a column on average): the week held 42.5 MB, 833 bytes a column; the busiest three hours, 1,080 columns, held 2.2 MB, about what a one-hour zone holds with its margins. The layer's reads stay in the tab's shared pool of four, so with the heatmap pulling a day of history they take their turn. The runtime keeps ten-second columns for hours and its store brings them back over a restart; the host keeps a week (`WINDOW_SPAN_MS` is held to its retention by a test), and a screen reaching further back than that has nothing to read for the part beyond, which the last line counts as having no data.

## Settings

Saved per instrument with the live-indicator extras (`liveRangeByInstrument`, an entry saved before a setting existed gets its default) and offered as presets under the instrument:

- **Mode**: Visible range, Selected zone or Live.
- **Big print from**: a slider on the instrument's own scale (`printScaleFor`, by how its sizes are named, like `sizeUnitFor`): coins from 0.5 to 50 in steps of 0.5, default 5 (`CRYPTO_PRINT_SCALE`); contracts from 1 to 300, default 10 (`FUTURES_PRINT_SCALE`); shares from 100 to 50,000 in steps of 100, default 5,000 (`EQUITY_PRINT_SCALE`). The label names the size and the unit ("Big print from 5 BTC"). A saved 0 means the instrument's default (`bigPrintOf`); the presets schema reads the setting on a grid every scale sits on.
- **Verdict sensitivity**: High, Normal or Strict as three radios, Normal by default (`VERDICT_SENSITIVITIES`): the z from which the verdict names a side, any z above or below 0, ±1 or ±2, once the two sides won five minutes between them. An unknown saved value reads as Normal (`verdictSensitivityOf`).
- **Window**, in the zone modes only: the window's two handles in Selected zone or its one handle in Live, labelled with the window and its length ("Window since 01:36 UTC · 59 min"). It is built from the browser's own sliders so it looks like the others in either theme: the start slider reads from the right, so its fill runs from the start towards now, and the end slider lies over it, cut at the middle between the handles, so a press goes to the nearer handle. The cut is put on a whole device pixel of the measured slider (`windowCut`), which keeps a seam from showing in the fill. There is no zone width.
- **Visibility**: the zone colour (blue by default) and its opacity (a slider from 1 to 50 %, 10 by default, `zoneOpacity`; the shade under the candles takes it, the lines keep the full colour), the bid and the ask colour (the heatmap's by default), the monitor size (Small, Medium or Large as three radios, Medium by default, `CARD_SIZES`), the rows (a tick each for Traded volume, Delta, Big prints, Minutes won and Price impact, all on by default), Show zone (on by default), Show hover tips (on by default) and Show on chart.

## Files

- `liveRangePrimitive.ts`: the series primitive: the mode's range, the timer, the report and its signature, and the zone's drawing, labels and price scale.
- `liveRangeGeometry.ts`: a moment's place on the chart and back, the candle length, the candles' time inside a range (`tradedSpans`), and the line under the pointer.
- `liveRangeWindow.ts`: the window over the last seven days, its slider minutes and its saved copy.
- `liveRangeReader.ts`: the trades of the range (`readLiveRange`, `sumColumn`, `seamOf`, `gridGaps`, `spanOf`), the noise, the minutes won, the price impact and the stretches with no data.
- `liveRangePrints.ts`: the big prints of the range (`PrintLedger`, and `readPrints`, the plain read it is checked against).
- `liveRangeHistory.ts`: the host's ten-second columns, their trade rows and their prints, kept per hour for the instrument, the hours far from the range let go.
- `liveRangeWords.ts`: the words of the card, the dialog and the legend, the rows and the verdict.
- `liveRangeSettings.ts`: the settings, the monitor sizes, the verdict sensitivities, the row ticks, the print scales and the presets schema.
- `LiveRangeCard.tsx`, `cardPosition.ts`: the card and its spot.
- `LiveRangeSettingsModal.tsx`: the dialog on the shared `IndicatorSettingsModal`, Inputs and Visibility.
- `liveRangeControlIndicator.ts`: the registry entry.

`MainPanel` keeps the settings per instrument and the window for all of them, owns the legend row ("the candles on screen", or the window in a zone mode) and the dialog, and hands the chart the live grid (`applyLiveRange`), the settings with the big print resolved for the instrument and its unit (`setLiveRangeSettings`), and the window (`setLiveRangeWindow`). `ChartPanel` owns the primitive (shown only while the indicator is on and the chart is live), gives it a lane of the column history for the host's columns, turns presses on the zone lines into drags and hands the dragged window up (`onLiveRangeWindowChange`), and renders the card.
