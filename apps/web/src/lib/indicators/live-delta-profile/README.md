# Live Delta Profile

Net market buys minus market sells at every price row since the trading day (or week) opened, drawn beside the price axis and moving with every trade. Purely L1: executed trades by aggressor side, never the book. Indicator id `liveDeltaProfile`, category L1.

## Files

- `deltaProfileStore.ts` — the rows and where they come from. `baseRowWidthFor` is the fine row each instrument is kept in (BTC $1, ETH $0.10, other crypto a cent, futures a tick, stocks a cent); the chart merges rows up from there for the timeframe and the zoom, so neither triggers a new ask.
- `profileWindow.ts` — the window the profile covers, six modes in the settings. **Auto** (the default) follows the chart: the last 0.5 h on 1m and 5m, the full day on higher timeframes (`autoWindowMode`, `AUTO_ROLLING_MAX_INTERVAL_MS`), so `windowFor` takes the chart's timeframe; a mode the trader picks sticks on every timeframe. **Full week**: since the trading week opened, Monday 00:00 UTC on crypto, Sunday 18:00 New York on futures, Monday midnight New York on stocks; the first load of a week costs the host about 20 s on BTC perp and up to a minute on ETH perp (today's pieces come first), after which the runtime's cache makes it instant. **Full day**: since the trading day opened, 00:00 UTC on crypto, 18:00 New York on futures (so the overnight is in), New York midnight on stocks. **Session**: the Killzones session the clock is in (Asia 20:00–00:00, London 02:00–05:00, NY AM 09:30–11:00, NY PM 13:30–16:00 New York, on every market); between sessions the last one stays, frozen, until the next opens. **Last N hours**: N in half hours from 0.5 to 24, moving on in half-hour steps, so the profile covers between N and N + 0.5 hours. **Visible range**: the candles on screen, to the minute, read 300 ms after the last scroll or zoom; open ended while the newest candle is in view, a frozen picture when the chart is back in history; no further back than the week the host keeps trades (the label says "nothing before <date>" when the view goes past it). The New York clock is the Killzones one.
- `liveDeltaProfileIndicator.ts` — the menu entry.
- `liveDeltaProfilePrimitive.ts` — the drawing: what the delta profile adds to the shared axis-profile primitive (`../axis-profile/`, which owns the two pane views, the geometry beside the axis, the easing, the animation loop, the right-offset room and `restoreRoom()`, the two grey handles and the `AxisNeighbour` chain). This file brings the store as the source, the merge factor for the timeframe and zoom, the rows placed by net delta, a bar's target width (its share, never shorter than its number), the last-trade row as the marker with the room it reserves right of the candles (`MARKER_GAP_PX`, `MARKER_ARROW_PX`), the flash per print (`FLASH_MS`), the status log, and the two draws: the bars under the candles, and the numbers and the marker above them. ChartPanel calls `restoreRoom()` after every snapshot whose range reaches the last candle (a timeframe switch in historical mode left the profile 8 px wide before this) and feeds `setNeighbour` from its `axisNeighbour()`: today the heatmap ladder (share of the pane, `ladderReach`) and Agha's Volume Profile OHLC (`profileWidth` px); with both on, the wider one counts. The primitive knows nothing about either; a new indicator drawn at the axis is one more entry in that list, with its width and its own resize bounds.
- `deltaProfileLayout.ts` — the pure part of the drawing: `mergeRows` sums fine rows into wider ones on the same absolute grid, `layoutDelta` places the rows in view with each bar's share of the biggest net in view, `holesIn` compares classified profile volume with comparable candle volume (`HOLE_SHARE`). For dxFeed it uses the candle's explicit buy-plus-sell volume, never total volume that may include unclassified prints; without explicit sides it cannot infer a recording hole. `profileStatus` + `StatusLog` put the profile's state in the browser console, never on the chart.
- `liveDeltaProfileSettings.ts` — per-instrument settings: row size (choices Auto, 1, 4, 12, 24, 40, 80 and 160 base rows; Auto = 1 on 1m, 4 on 5m, 12 on 15m, 40 on 1h, 120 on 4h, 400 on 1D, merged further while a drawn row would be under `MIN_ROW_PX`), the longest bar's reach as a share of the chart, numbers on or off, colours, opacity. Saved with the other live-indicator extras.
- `candleDeltaSource.ts` — the `DeltaSource` interface the primitive reads (the store satisfies it) and `CandleDeltaSource`, the source on a historical chart: no trades there, so each candle on screen spreads its buys and sells evenly over the rows between its low and high (`rowsFromCandles`), the marker sits on the last candle's close, and the window is always what is on screen. An estimate, said once in the console; Binance candles carry the side split, Databento candles get it from Agha's `bar_flow` study. ChartPanel hands it to the primitive only when historical capabilities are set and there is no live store (`candleDeltaSource()`), and re-feeds it on every snapshot and bar-flow merge. Feeds are never named: any archive whose candles carry buy volume works (registry policy `CANDLE_FLOW_POLICY`).
- `liveDeltaProfileNumbers.ts` — `formatDelta`, the net of a row as the bar's number, rounded to a whole number. There is no hover card: the trader asked for the numbers on the bars instead, so the profile costs the browser nothing on mouse moves.
- `LiveDeltaProfileSettingsModal.tsx` — the settings dialog (Inputs: row size, reach, numbers; Visibility: colours, opacity, show on chart).

## What the bar shows

One bar per drawn row, from the price axis leftwards. Its length is the row's net (market buys minus market sells) against the biggest net in view, the longest bar stopping `BAR_EDGE_GAP_PX` short of the grey line; but never shorter than its own number without the sign (`barPx`: about 0.62 em per character plus 8 px, so "12" and "-12" get 22 px at the full font and "5" and "-5" 15 px; the colour carries the side), the trader's rule that 10 and up fits two digits and under 10 fits one, and the number sits inside the bar when it clears it by `INSIDE_MARGIN_PX` (`fitsInside`), so the small bars stop being proportional and read instead; blue when buyers won, red when sellers won. The net is written beside the bars in the chart's own font and on a pill of the chart's own background colour (`chartLook()` reads `layout.fontFamily` and `layout.background` off the chart, so the numbers match the axis and read in a dark or a light theme without a setting): inside the bar at the axis end when the bar is wide enough, otherwise just left of the bar. The font follows the row height (`labelFontPx`, 11 px down to 8 px). `placeLabels` gives every bar with a net its number, small ones included, biggest first, and skips only a bar whose number would sit on one already placed (so zoomed out, the big ones win the space). The number is a whole number (`formatDelta`: 70.6 reads 71, k from 10,000 as in 5913k, M from 10,000k, "<1" when a net rounds to nothing), the same in every unit. The row of the last print carries an arrow next to the last candle and a dotted line to the axis. Two grey lines frame the profile and both are handles. The left one, down the profile's edge, changes the profile's reach when dragged (the same `widthShare` as the settings slider, 5% to 60% of the pane). The one at the base, where the bars start, sits on the neighbour's left edge while something is drawn against the axis, and dragging it resizes that neighbour through its own setting: the heatmap's `ladderReach` (5% to 80%, `onLiquidityLadderReachChange`) or the OHLC profile's `profileWidth` (50 to 300 px in steps of 10, `onVolumeDeltaProfileOhlcWidthChange`); with nothing beside it, the base line is just the profile's edge against the axis and does nothing. So the trader can make room for the heatmap, shrink the ladder, or widen the bars; ChartPanel captures the pointer on the line and swallows the pointerdown so the chart never starts its own drag, then reports the new share through `onLiveDeltaReachChange` (never toggle the chart's scroll/scale options for this, that froze the chart). Nothing about loading or missing history is drawn or put in the legend (the trader's rule): the browser console gets one line each time the state changes, `Live Delta Profile: loading history`, `history complete`, `history busy, retrying`, `history unavailable (READ_FAILED)`, `history refused: rows too fine for this instrument`, or `partial, the recording is missing 14:00–20:00`. The legend reads `N rows · <window>`.

## Where the trades come from

The store keeps one invariant: rows = the host's trades before the cut (`liveFrom`) plus the browser's own trade frames from the cut on, both summed over the current window only. The cut is set when the store first gets a window, just after the newest print it has been shown (the snapshot's trades, `markSeen`, and the live ones that came before the window), on the exchange's clock, so a browser clock off the exchange's cannot open a hole between the two sources; the browser's clock is used only when no print has been seen at all. A live print older than the cut is ignored: it is the host's. The host is asked, through the runtime's `delta_profile` request, only for the parts of the window before the cut that no piece covers yet, and only once `DELTA_ASK_DELAY_MS` (5 s) has passed since the cut was set, so the recorder has written up to it. Nothing is counted twice and nothing is dropped between the two sources.

The store keeps every host piece and every minute slice of live prints with its own rows, whatever window they were fetched for, and `rows()` sums only those inside the current window. So a window that moves (a new day, a sliding hour, a pan in visible range) reads what it already has and asks the host only for the gaps (`gapsIn`, newest first); a piece cut by a new window edge is dropped and asked again, so pieces never overlap. The reply comes in two-hour pieces (half-hour pieces for the sliding and visible windows, asked with `piece_ms`), newest first, each adjacent to the last within its request; `complete` turns true when no gap is left. A closed window (a session, or a view back in history) takes no live print past its close and is asked only up to it. A reconnect (`reanchor`) drops everything and forgets what it had seen, so the next window sets the cut after the reconnect's snapshot and whatever the drop missed is the host's. Pieces are bounded to 1,024 (the farthest from the window go first) and live slices to two days (`MAX_LIVE_SLICES`): past that the oldest minutes are dropped up to the next two-hour mark and the cut moves there, so the host answers that stretch as one piece. A failed piece ends the request; the remainder is asked again after a backoff (30 s, 60 s, then ten minutes; a server `retry_after_ms` is honoured), an unanswered request expires after 90 s as a failure, and `ROWS_CAPPED` is final (the row is too fine for this instrument); `BAD_REQUEST` (the runtime could not accept the request) is retried like the other reasons, so a later window can still load. The rows keep their `version` for the renderer; React only learns of pieces and issues (`deltaProfileVersion`), never of single trades.

`MainPanel` drives it while the indicator is on: every 5 s (and at once when the settings or, in visible range, the view change) it sets the window for the instrument and mode and forwards the next request, like the band-history driver.

`Volume Profile (Ticks)` reads the same trades over a window of its own, always the visible range. It keeps a second store (`deltaTickStore` in `useMarketData`; its request ids start a stride up, `DELTA_REQUEST_ID_STRIDE`, so the one reply channel is never ambiguous), fed the same snapshot and live prints; `MainPanel` sets its window with `visibleWindowFor` whenever the indicator is on, starting from the range on screen (`getVisibleRangeMs`) rather than waiting for the chart to report a change of it.

The two drivers share the one request channel, and the host serves **one delta_profile job per connection**: a newer request cancels the older one and sends nothing for it (`delta-profile.js`, "only the newest request per client is served"). Left to ask freely, each store's request would cancel the other's job, and the cancelled store would sit on its request until it expired at 90 s. So each driver asks only while the other store has no request out (`deltaChannelFree`), and each re-runs when the other's version moves, so a freed channel is taken at once. The second store doubles the live slices held (up to `MAX_LIVE_SLICES` each) and can ask the host for windows the first already holds; the stores keep their own windows, so nothing is shared between them. That is what replaced reading the profile off the heatmap ladder, whose columns only reach back two hours: the host holds the trades, so the profile can cover the whole view, and it reports the same "profile from ..." coverage while the host is still answering.

Its row count follows the `Fixed amount of rows` toggle. Off (the default), the visible candles' price range is divided into `binCount` rows (20 to 1,500) only while each is at least one of the source's own rows tall; past that each bucket is one whole source row, so zooming in shows fewer, real rows rather than one row smeared flat. On, the profile is exactly `binCount` rows at any zoom, and each source row's flow is spread over the buckets its band reaches. The POC and the value area are then read off those rows *after* the neighbour blend that smooths the silhouette, never off the rows as they arrived: the blend gives a narrow spike half its height to its neighbours, so a broader lower bulge can be drawn taller than it, and a line placed on the pre-blend row would sit beside the bulge it names.

On a historical chart the tick profile reads `CandleDeltaSource`, the same candle-derived estimate the Live Delta Profile falls back to: each candle's volume spread evenly from its low to its high, not executed trades. The source says so (`estimated`), and the primitive draws a status badge, "Volume Profile (Ticks) · estimated from candles" (`STATUS_BADGE_SLOT.volumeProfileTickEstimate`), whenever it draws one.

The source's version moves on every print, and a rebuild reads every row the source holds (about 9 ms over a week of two-hour pieces). So a change that is only new trades rebuilds at most every `TICK_PROFILE_LIVE_REBUILD_MS` (250 ms), with one repaint owed at the end of the interval so the latest prints still land. A moved window, price domain or setting rebuilds at once.

## Measured (2026-09-26, against the host)

A day of BTC perp is 1.5 M trades (1-3 s); a week is 18 M (BTC) and 26.6 M (ETH) trades, refused in one request (503 at 8 s) and read in two-hour pieces of at most ~2 s. NQ reads a day in under a second. A week comes back as 2-7 k rows.

Holes found in the host recording: Binance 2026-09-23 14:00-20:00 UTC; dxFeed NQ/ES from Sunday 2026-09-21 22:00 to Monday 09-22 14:00 UTC. The store keeps each piece's traded volume (`pieces`) so the log can compare it with the candles' volume and name a hole instead of reading it as zero.
