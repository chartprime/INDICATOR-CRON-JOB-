# Historical Tardis incremental-book input

`historical-tardis-frames.js` consumes rows from one retained Binance spot or USDT-perpetual `incremental_book_L2` stream in provider file order. The observed schema is `exchange,symbol,timestamp,local_timestamp,is_snapshot,side,price,amount`; timestamps are decimal **microseconds**. The translator retains the exact microsecond timestamp pair in its result and floors exchange time to milliseconds only for the current engine frame.

Consecutive rows with the same `(timestamp, local_timestamp, is_snapshot)` form one book change. The August 5 retained files start with `is_snapshot=true` groups containing about 2,000–2,500 rows, followed by `false` groups of changes. A snapshot group replaces the entire local book and requires a fresh engine segment. A `false` group applies absolute level sizes; amount zero deletes a level. The resulting book is checked only **after the full group**, because an intermediate row may temporarily cross the spread. Incremental frames carry the changed levels and the reconstructed best bid/ask, avoiding a full-book copy on every update.

Zero-size rows also occur **inside the opening snapshot group**. They are absent levels and must be ignored. On August 5, excluding them made the opening full book match all 25 bids and 25 asks in the independent `book_snapshot_25` file for BTC and ETH spot and perpetual. Applying the first following incremental group then matched the next 25-level snapshot for all four products at the same timestamp pair. A bounded read-only reconstruction of the first 1,000 groups per product found 807, 753, 711 and 777 timestamp-matched wide snapshots respectively, with no top-25 mismatch or crossed/empty book. The wide file samples some groups rather than every group; an oracle joins by the full timestamp pair. A full-day pilot must compare more points and check every file's integrity and continuity.

The complete BTC spot August 5 comparison does **not** pass: 1,476 of 632,525 timestamp-matched wide snapshots disagree across 39 runs, beginning at 19:47 UTC. The reconstruction produced no crossed/empty group, and the exchange-native `book_ticker` agrees with its best bid/ask at the first disagreement, but that check cannot certify deeper levels. There are three complete incremental snapshot groups on the day, all before the first disagreement. The day remains unqualified; do not derive or publish it until the conflicting source observations have a defensible trust policy and bounded gap treatment.

The translator refuses mismatched identity, malformed numbers, receive-time regression, duplicate live snapshot levels and unbounded timestamp groups. It bounds the retained book to 20,000 levels per side by default, matching the engine ledger's default record cap; exceeding that bound closes trust with `BOOK_LEVEL_LIMIT`. Without a complete snapshot, it emits no READY book. An empty or crossed result closes trust with `INVALID_BOOK` and stays unavailable until a later complete snapshot. `historical-source-runner.js` enforces the requested fresh engine segment; its caller must also close at missing days, changed object identities, and source-file boundaries that lack a verified stitch. These modules alone do not establish continuous coverage or align independent trade rows.
