# Live Order Book Profile

The book as it stands now, one bar per price band beside the price axis, bids below the spread and asks above. It reads the heatmap's newest column and nothing else: no history, no trail. It draws with the heatmap on or off, and stays nearest the candles when other profiles sit at the axis.

## Files

- `bookProfileSource.ts` — where the bars come from. `bookBands(column, rowWidth, clusterRows, style, wider)` turns the latest liquidity column into bands of `clusterRows` rows on the grid's absolute row index (a band never moves as the view scrolls), each read through the instrument's own `LiquidityStyle.bandSizes`: summed rows on crypto, the biggest row on futures and stocks, the same read the heatmap ladder makes, so the two can never disagree. Past the finest column's window (2,048 rows on the wire, 512 points on NQ) the profile reads the wider zoom lanes' live columns the way the heatmap ladder does (`widerLanes`, finest lane first; a lane whose rows are not a whole multiple of the grid's is skipped): a band the finest column covers is never overwritten by a lane; a lane whose rows divide the cluster gives the same bands at the same index, otherwise the lane's own rows are drawn at their own prices (`index` is the band they fall in). The bands come back in price order. Until 2026-10-12 the profile stopped at the finest window while the ladder beside it went on, so the book looked shallower than the heatmap's. Empty bands are left out, and a side that would read under one unit (it rounds to nothing, the "<1" of `formatDelta`) counts as empty, so an almost empty level stays empty instead of drawing a sliver marked "<1" (the trader asked for it on 2026-09-29, on BTC spot where far rows hold hundredths of a coin); rows that add up to one or more in a band still show. A band the spread sits in keeps both sides. `spreadOf` is the highest bid row and the lowest ask row. `bookExtent` is the lowest and the highest row holding anything at all, however small, over the finest column and the lanes, in the finest rows (`BookProfileSource.extent()`), so the spine runs as deep as the ladder. `isBookLive` is the dead-book rule: a column older than `BOOK_STALE_MS` (5 s) is not drawn as live, so a stalled server or a crossed book never shows as a standing book. `BookProfileSource` wraps a `LiquidityGrid` and the chart's zoom lanes (`HeatLanes`, the `L2Store`'s `lanes`, given at construction by MainPanel or through `setLanes`): `bands(clusterRows)` reads `grid.latest` and the lanes' latest columns fresh and keeps the result only until the grid's `version`, a lane's `version` or the cluster changes; `live(now)` reads the latest column each time. `spread(now)` is the best bid row and best ask row from the `book` frame when one was marked within `BOOK_STALE_MS` (`markBook(book, now)`, which MainPanel calls with every book frame: ten a second at most, against the column once a second plus its way here), else the latest column. A book frame counts only when it is `READY`, not `stale`, carries both sides and names the grid's own instrument (symbol, provider and market kind: Binance spot and perp share a symbol); its prices go on the grid's rows through `rowOfPrice`, rounded through twelve digits first so a fractional row width lands on the right row. `version` moves with the grid's, with the lanes' and once more each time the marked mid changes rows, so the profile repaints for a moving mid and not for every frame. Nothing else accumulates, so out-of-order columns and resets cannot leave it wrong.

- `liveOrderBookProfileIndicator.ts` — the menu entry: id `liveOrderBookProfile`, "Live Order Book Profile", category L2. Unavailable in historical mode (the archive keeps candles, not the book).
- `liveOrderBookProfileSettings.ts` — per-instrument settings, saved with the live-indicator extras (`liveOrderBookByInstrument`): row size (0 = the heatmap's own bands for the instrument and timeframe, through `clusterRowsFor`; otherwise that many book rows, choices 1 to 32), the longest bar's reach as a share of the chart (same bounds as the delta profile), numbers on or off, bid and ask colours (they start on the heatmap's), the spread marker colour, opacity. `clusterRowsOf` picks the cluster, and nothing else changes it: the rows change with this setting (and, on the heatmap's own bands, with the timeframe, as the heatmap's do), never with the zoom. A crypto row reads the sum of its orders, so rows that merged as the chart zoomed out changed the numbers with the zoom: on 2026-09-28 a 200 BTC wall at 82,272 read 402 in a $48 row and 333 in a $40 row two zoom steps apart.
- `LiveOrderBookProfileSettingsModal.tsx` — the settings dialog. Inputs, each named in a few plain words with no explanation under it (the trader asked for a retail dialog): Row size, Profile width, Show sizes, Show big orders, Big order size, Show after, Big order bar length. Visibility: colours, opacity, show on chart.

- `bookProfileLayout.ts` — the pure part of the drawing: `layoutBook` places the bands in view as bars, each with its share of the biggest size in view; a band the spread sits in becomes two bars, the bid on its lower half and the ask on its upper half; `spreadPrice` is the middle of the gap between best bid and best ask; `bookRowsReadable` is the zoom line: rows at least `MIN_LABEL_FONT_PX + 2` px tall (10 px) fit a number, the same line the numbers use. Zoomed out below it the profile scales down gradually with the row height (`bookColumnPx`): every level at least the stub (`bookStubPx`, three characters wide at the row's font), the biggest level the room times the row height over the readable height, never under `ZOOMED_OUT_REACH` (a third) of the room, and the others in proportion, so there is no jump at the line (a hard switch to a third there, the second version, read as a different chart one zoom step apart), so an 80 still reads bigger than a 12 and the width setting still sets the column's width, while the only bars that run far into the chart are the big resting orders; the trader found hundreds of hairlines of different lengths at that zoom harsh, and a flat column (the first version) lost the sizes and the slider (2026-09-28). Zoomed in, `bookBarPx` is a bar's length: its share of the room, but never narrower than its own number (`LABEL_CHAR_EM` per character of the number at the row's font plus `LABEL_PAD_PX`, the delta profile's rule), so every level with size is a bar wide enough to carry its number, and the small resting levels that come and go read as bars beside the big walls instead of hairlines (2026-09-28, after the trader's DOM-ladder example). `bookLabels` picks the rows that get a number when rows are thinner than one: the biggest first (the lower index on a tie), each taking the font's height around its row's middle, and a row whose number would overlap one already placed goes without; on rows tall enough every row gets its number.
- `liveOrderBookProfilePrimitive.ts` — what the book adds to the shared axis-profile primitive (`../axis-profile/`): the grid source through `BookProfileSource`, the cluster for the timeframe (the heatmap's own, so `setHeatmapSettings` gets the heatmap's settings from ChartPanel) never merged further by the zoom (zoomed out the rows only get thinner), the spread as the marker, a thin strip against the edge (`BOOK_SPINE_PX`, 3 px) over the whole book from `extent()`, in the ask colour above the spread and the bid colour below, drawn under the bars, so rows that hold nothing or under one unit keep the profile one continuous shape instead of a book with holes (the trader asked for it on 2026-09-29, after empty levels stopped drawing "<1" slivers), and the look: one solid silhouette, rows touching, bids in the bid colour and asks in the ask colour at the settings' opacity (1 by default); the size in white right against the axis (`LABEL_INSET_PX`) on every row there is room for, through `bookLabels`, so zoomed out the big rows keep their numbers and the small ones go without, and a number is always its own row's size, in the chart's font, as a whole number the way the delta profile writes its net (`formatDelta`: 9.8 reads 10, "<1" under half a unit, k from 10,000); the spread marked the way the delta profile marks its last trade (the shared `drawRowMarker`): an arrow next to the last candle pointing at the middle of the gap between best bid and best ask (from the book frame while it is fresh, so the arrow moves with the book and not a second and more behind it like the column, which is where the delta profile's arrow was visibly ahead of it), a dotted line from it to the axis and a small tick at the axis end, in the spread colour; the profile reserves the arrow's room right of the candles like the delta profile does. The longest bar stops `BAR_EDGE_GAP_PX` (12 px) short of the profile's edge. The two resize lines are not drawn until the pointer is on one (`_handlesAlwaysShown` false; ChartPanel calls `setHandleHover` as the pointer moves), so the profile has no frame on the chart; they still drag as before. No numbers pill; every bar is at least as wide as its number, so the number always sits on its own bar. A book older than `BOOK_STALE_MS` draws nothing; the console gets one line when the book goes stale and one when columns resume, never the chart.

`MainPanel` keeps one settings object per instrument, builds a `BookProfileSource` over the live grid and the `L2Store`'s lanes, hands it to the chart while the indicator is on (`applyOrderBookProfile`, `setLiveOrderBookSettings`), and puts `N rows` in the legend while the book is live, `waiting for the book` while it is not. `ChartPanel` creates the primitive beside the delta profile's, feeds it the heatmap's settings on every visibility pass, and sets its neighbour: the Live Delta Profile while that one is on (its whole width, through `widthPx`; dragging the book's base line then changes the delta profile's reach through `reachShareForWidth` and `onLiveDeltaReachChange`), otherwise the widest of the heatmap ladder, the OHLC profile and the Book Map profile, as the delta profile does. So the order at the axis is always axis, ladder or OHLC or Book Map, delta profile, book profile, candles. Both profiles are on ChartPanel's axis occupant list at their drawn width (`frame.width - frame.right + frame.reach`), so order blocks and FVG boxes stop before the book profile; neither is picked as a neighbour. The pointer handlers know which profile's line they hold; the book's edge line reports through `onLiveOrderBookReachChange` into the same `widthShare` as the settings slider. Historical mode never applies a source, so the book draws nothing there.

## Big resting orders

The second half of the indicator: the big limit orders resting in the book are drawn as bars from the price axis into the chart, at their price, and fade when they go, pulled or eaten alike. Passive orders only; icebergs are another indicator's business. It was a flash on landing first (2026-09-28); the trader found the flashes jumpy for a retail reader and asked for something static, so it now shows what rests, however long it has been there.

- `restingOrders.ts` — the detector, pure. `stepRestingOrders(state, column, floor, epoch)` reads one column: a row at or above the floor is tracked from the column it first holds it, on either side. The floor is `restingFloor(minSize, reference)`: the minimum size the trader set in the book's own units (BTC on BTC, contracts on NQ, shares on a stock), or, at 0 ("auto", the default), `RESTING_MULTIPLE` times `reference`, the book's own yardstick: the smoothed 95th percentile row near price that the grid keeps on every column (`column.reference`), so one multiple reads every instrument and every hour of the day the same way; `RESTING_MULTIPLE` is 3, measured on the 24 h stores (at 3× the whole book holds about 10-20 such rows at once on crypto and 20-60 on the stocks; within 1 % of price it is 2-4 on BTC perp, 0-3 on ES and ETH, 7-14 on NQ and the stocks; on the perps they rest 30-60 s in the median, minutes elsewhere). A tracked order shows once it has stood the wait the trader set (`restingStandS`, 3 s to 2 min; `RESTING_STAND_MS` 30 s by default), so a requote that comes and goes is never drawn. Measured on the 24 h stores at 3×: with a 3 s wait three in four bars on BTC perp and six in seven on ETH perp are gone again within 30 s; at 30 s it is one in four on the perps and under one in seven elsewhere; at 60 s under one in eight everywhere, but a wall only shows a minute after it lands. The wait applies at once, without starting over: an order that has already stood long enough shows on the next column, and one that no longer qualifies is simply taken off, with no flash, as after a change of the multiple. It follows the row's size while it rests, and goes when the row has held under half of its peak (`RESTING_END_SHARE`) for `RESTING_END_HOLD_MS` (2 s), so a partial refill does not blink it off and on. Whether price ate it or the owner pulled it makes no difference to the drawing. Nothing is stored: a new grid epoch drops everything. `RestingOrderTracker` wraps the state for the chart: `update(grid, minSize, standMs)` walks the grid's new fine columns since its last look and gives what is shown; it starts over with the grid's epoch and when the minimum size changes. A change of instrument brings a new grid, so a new `BookProfileSource` and a new tracker.
- Whenever the tracker starts over (the first look, a new grid epoch, a new minimum size) it replays the last `RESTING_REPLAY_MS` (125 s, more than the longest wait the settings allow) of the grid's columns, then walks only what arrives after. So a wall that was already standing shows at once after a reload, the profile switched off and on, the book coming back from stale or a change of the minimum size, instead of making the trader wait out the whole wait again (the first version started from the newest column, and every dxFeed token rotation or toggle blanked the bars for the full wait). `BookProfileSource.resting(minSize, standMs)` owns one tracker per grid; `typicalSize()` is the latest column's yardstick, for the dialog. Nothing restarts it from outside: while the profile is off or the book stale the tracker is simply not asked, and when asked again it walks the columns it missed, so an order keeps its true `since` across the gap.
- `restingOrderLayout.ts` — the timing and geometry, pure and on the wall clock. `reconcileResting` keeps the bars on show: a new order is stamped when first seen, one no longer shown is stamped as ended, and an ended one is forgotten after `RESTING_FADE_MS` (2 s); an order that rests again on a row still fading starts over. `restingAlpha` grows in over `RESTING_GROW_MS` (300 ms), stands full, and from the moment the order goes fades to nothing over two seconds. `restingUntil` is when the last bar stops moving (the end of its appearance or of its fade), which keeps the profile's animation loop repainting until then and no longer. `layoutResting` merges the orders into the profile's own bands (`clusterIndex` of the row at the profile's cluster, which the zoom never changes), so two big rows in one band are one bar, and reads each bar's size from the profile's band (`sizeOf`, the band's size as the profile shows it: summed on crypto, the biggest row on futures and stocks), so the number on the bar is always the number the profile shows for that band. Every order under a drawn bar remembers the size the bar was drawn at while it stood (`drawn`, written on the `RestingShown` entry by the layout, 0 until its bar is drawn). An order under half its peak is on its way out (`ending`, from the tracker's `under`): its bar holds at that size for the two seconds the tracker waits, instead of collapsing to what the profile has left in the band, and a bar whose orders have all gone or are all going fades at that size: it lights up and fades in place, at the length it had, not at what the profile has left there and not at the orders' own peaks. An order whose bar was never drawn (cut by the count below) ends without a trace. Each bar covers its band, never thinner than `RESTING_MIN_HEIGHT_PX` (8 px); what is off the pane is left out; the largest `RESTING_SHOWN_MAX` (6) standing bars on screen are kept, biggest first, and a bar whose orders are going or gone keeps its place outside that count until it has faded (cut during the hold, it would vanish for two seconds and come back to fade), each with its `share` of the biggest, so the lengths read as sizes against what is on screen. The first version sized a fading band at its orders' own peaks and counted it among the six: a wall's own size is smaller than its band, so a pulled wall lost its place the frame it ended, and the flash and fade ran on a bar that was no longer drawn (headless-Chrome sampling on 2026-09-28: five pulls, none drawn after their end). `restingFlash` is the flash a bar gets as it appears (full the moment it is first shown, settling to its colour over `RESTING_APPEAR_MS`, 1 s, while it grows in, so a wall that has just landed catches the eye and then stays; the trader asked for it on 2026-09-28, like the pull but staying) and again the moment its order goes, pulled or eaten alike: full at that moment, gone after `RESTING_FLASH_MS` (300 ms); `restingFill` lifts the bar's colour towards white by `RESTING_FLASH_LIFT` (60 %) of the flash, so a bar lights up as it starts to fade.
- The drawing is in two parts. The bar itself is the last thing the profile's bottom view paints, so it sits over the profile's bars and the Live Delta Profile's but under the candles: price is never hidden, and a bar the candles run through reads as an order in front of price. It runs from the profile's own right edge (`frame.right`: the price axis, or the gap before the Live Delta Profile when that sits between the book and the axis; a bar never runs over the delta profile) into the chart: the biggest order on screen `restingShare` of the pane long (a fifth by default, from a sliver of 1 % that leaves only the numbers to half the pane), the others their share of that, in the bid or ask colour at the profile's opacity (the first version ignored it, so the big bars stayed solid while the ladder dimmed); a bar is never shorter than its number. Whatever the setting, no bar runs more than the last `RESTING_REACH_MS` (15 min) of price action past the last candle: the free space right of it plus that many minutes of candles (`frame.candleRight`, `frame.barSpacing` and `frame.intervalMs`, which the base hands every profile), so on 1m a bar can run fifteen candles into price, on 15m one, and on 1h and above it stops at the last candle. A resting order is about now, so it never lies across old candles like a heatmap wall, at any timeframe, and scrolled back in time, with the last candle past the axis, the bars draw back until only their numbers are left. The first version put the bars at a fixed share of the pane and lay across old candles whenever the chart moved; the second counted candles, which was a hundred minutes on 1m and four days on 1h. The axis end is the last thing the top view paints: a stub the width of the number, in the same colour, with the size as a whole number in white, over the profile's own number for that row; no bar is shorter than its stub. A stale book clears the bars, as it clears the profile; the tracker is not restarted, and walks the columns it missed once the book is live again.
- Settings, in the same dialog, under the "Bigger order S/R zones" heading with two circles, On and Off, laid out like the row size (`showResting`, on by default; off leaves the plain profile): `restingMinSize` ("Min size", 0 = auto, shown as the size auto means on this book right now; the slider runs to `restingSizeRange(typicalSize)`, `RESTING_SIZE_SPAN` 12 times the book's typical row rounded up to 1-2-5, at least 10, in steps of 10 from 1,000), `restingStandS` ("Show after", 3 to 120 s, 30 by default) and `restingShare` ("Bar length", 1 % to 50 %, 20 % by default). The min size was a multiple of the typical row until 2026-09-29; the trader wanted the number in the book's units, and thought the multiple changed how levels were grouped (it never did, row size does). Adding or renaming them resets a saved book-profile entry to the defaults once, since a saved entry has to carry every field.
