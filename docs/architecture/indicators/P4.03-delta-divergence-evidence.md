# Delta Divergence (P4.03)

> **Historical implementation evidence.** This dated page records the result
> of a completed build or audit. It is not a current deployment/health
> statement; use the handbook and final-state overview for supported operation.

Delta Divergence is a **research annotation**. It highlights a confirmed price
extension whose nearby cumulative-volume-delta (CVD) structure does not
confirm it. It is not a reversal forecast, an entry/exit instruction, or a
claim about participant intent.

## Product contract

The user has one behavioural control: **Swing length**, from 2 to 8 completed
bars (default 3).

- Lower values identify structure faster and produce more annotations.
- Higher values require broader, more selective price structure.
- There is no confidence threshold, statistical population gate, or hidden
  signal score. A valid confirmed divergence is shown rather than being
  suppressed by an opaque ranking model.

The CVD timing tolerance is deliberately derived rather than exposed as a
second setting:

`min(3, max(1, floor(swingLength / 2)))` bars on each side of a price pivot.

This keeps the indicator understandable while allowing order flow to peak or
trough slightly before or after the accepted price close.

## Deterministic methodology

1. Only `closed: true` bars participate. An unfinished live bar can never
   confirm a historical pivot.
2. A price pivot high/low is a **strict extreme of candle closes** over
   `Swing length` completed bars on both sides. Wicks are intentionally not
   used: the method measures accepted closing structure, not a transient
   intrabar excursion.
3. Price pivots are the anchors. Around each anchor, the algorithm searches
   the derived timing window for the relevant CVD extremum:
   - price high -> local CVD high;
   - price low -> local CVD low.
4. The local CVD extremum is deterministic: most extreme value, then nearest
   to the price anchor, then earliest timestamp. One CVD point may not stand
   for both price pivots in a candidate pair.
5. A bearish divergence requires consecutive confirmed price close highs where
   the second is higher and the matched CVD high is lower **or equal**.
6. A bullish divergence requires consecutive confirmed price close lows where
   the second is lower and the matched CVD low is higher **or equal**.
7. A candidate is unavailable—not neutral—when the pair or either timing
   window touches a CVD gap. CVD is never compared across cumulative epochs.

The right-hand pivot-confirmation window creates an intentional signal delay.
For the default Swing length of 3, a price pivot is known only after three
following completed bars. The derived CVD window is ±1 bar and is therefore
already complete at that point.

## Rendering contract

- The price pane joins the two accepted closing pivots.
- The CVD pane joins the independently time-aligned CVD extrema; its line may
  lead or lag the price line by the permitted small window.
- The colour is red for bearish divergence and green for bullish divergence.
- The translucent band represents the period between the two price anchors.
- CVD-pane annotations require Aggressive Order Flow to be enabled; price-pane
  annotations remain available without displaying the CVD pane.

## Provider provenance and limits

The divergence rule is provider-neutral only after the common `BarFrame`
boundary. Its underlying flow measurement is provider-specific:

- **Binance:** `buy_volume` is exchange-supplied kline taker-buy base volume.
- **dxFeed:** `buy_volume` is constructed from eligible, terminal-classified
  TimeAndSale prints. dxFeed documents `aggressorSide`, `validTick`, and
  cancellation/correction semantics on its
  [TimeAndSale event](https://docs.dxfeed.com/dxfeed/api/com/dxfeed/event/market/TimeAndSale.html).

See [P4.02 Aggressive Order Flow](P4.02-aggressive-order-flow-evidence.md)
for the full source, gap, and comparability contract. A Binance CVD reading
and a dxFeed CVD reading must not be treated as directly interchangeable.

## Verification obligations

Deterministic tests must prove close-vs-wick behaviour, confirmation delay,
bearish/bullish/flat-CVD cases, nearby CVD lead/lag, CVD-gap rejection,
malformed input rejection, and Binance/dxFeed frame parity. Before release,
retain separate live Binance and dxFeed screenshots or replay evidence showing
the source provenance, CVD gap behaviour, pivots, and rendered annotations.

## Qualification reasons

Each `Divergence` carries a `reasons` array of
`DivergenceQualificationReason` values — `CONFIRMED_CLOSE_SWINGS`,
`SAME_CVD_EPOCH`, `CVD_ALIGNED_WITHIN_TOLERANCE`, `PRICE_HIGHER_HIGH`,
`PRICE_LOWER_LOW`, `CVD_LOWER_OR_EQUAL_HIGH`, `CVD_HIGHER_OR_EQUAL_LOW`.

These are **stable, inspectable facts about why a signal qualified**, for audit
and evidence tooling. They are not a trading instruction and carry no
directional recommendation: they record which conditions the detector actually
tested and satisfied, so a signal on the chart can be traced back to the
specific checks that produced it.

## Why the detector only ever sees completed candles

A pivot is only knowable from **fully completed** candles. Confirming one from a
live bar would let a historical signal appear and then retract as that bar
finished forming, so `findSwingHighs`/`findSwingLows` are close-based and
confirmed left and right. The names are retained for the existing public module
API even though both now mean "confirmed close-based pivot".

Two consequences worth stating, because both are deliberate:

- **The tie-break is stable, not arbitrary.** When the CVD series has a flat
  local top or bottom, the aligned point is chosen by closest-then-earliest, so
  the same inputs always produce the same divergence rather than one that moves
  between runs.
- **A timing window touching unknown flow is unavailable, not approximated.**
  Selecting the post-gap CVD value would compare two separate cumulative epochs
  (see P4.02's CVD epoch rules), which is why the detector declines instead.
  Likewise a single CVD extremum cannot stand in for two different price swings.
